# Reading a week

The numbers are the artifact. This page is how to avoid drawing the wrong conclusion from them.

## Before concluding anything

Two checks, every week, in this order:

1. **Did the inputs actually run?** A channel at zero usually means nobody sent anything, not that demand collapsed. Check the send logs before the funnel.
2. **Is any definition different from last week?** A changed filter produces a dramatic chart and a false finding.

Most alarming weeks are explained by one of these two.

## Where the drop is

Find the highest stage in the chain that moved. Everything below it is a consequence, not a finding.

| Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|
| Traffic down, everything else down | Input problem. One cause, not five. |
| Traffic flat, calls booked down | A conversion problem on the site or in the outbound message |
| Booked up, held flat | No-show problem. Operational: reminders, booking distance, who confirms. |
| Held up, fit down | Targeting. The message is reaching the wrong people, which usually means it got broader. |
| Fit up, signed flat | The sales conversation, or pricing. Look at the calls themselves. |
| Everything flat for three weeks | The actual steady state. Not a crisis, and not a reason to change four things at once. |

## Small numbers

A small firm's weekly funnel is mostly noise. Two calls instead of five is a 60% drop and usually means nothing.

Rules that keep this honest:

- Under 10 of anything per week, do not read percentage changes at all. Read counts and the four-week trend.
- One week is never a trend. Three consecutive weeks in the same direction is.
- A single large deal distorts revenue for a quarter. Note it in the row rather than explaining it repeatedly.

## Concentration

Each week, check what share of calls came from the single biggest channel. Over roughly two thirds is a risk worth naming even in a great week, because that channel will eventually change and the recovery time is months.

The same applies per sender where more than one person does outbound.

## What the review is not for

- **Deciding this week's actions.** That is a pipeline conversation, and mixing the two turns a thirty-minute review into a two-hour meeting.
- **Judging individual performance.** Per-sender numbers exist to find which message works, not to rank people, and the moment they are used for ranking the logging quality collapses.
- **Forecasting.** The history supports a forecast; it is not one.

## When the numbers are bad

Write them down anyway, with the note. A history with gaps in the bad weeks is worthless, because the bad weeks are exactly where the lesson is, and the gap is always in the same place: the quarter someone would most want to look back at.
