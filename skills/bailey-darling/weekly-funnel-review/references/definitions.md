# Definitions and sources

Written down once, then not renegotiated. The point is not that these definitions are the best ones; it is that they stop moving.

## The chain

| Stage | What it counts | Must exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic | Sessions to the site | Bots, internal and QA traffic, local development |
| Outbound activity | Messages sent, by channel and by sender | Automated follow-ups already counted at send |
| Inbound and referral | Enquiries not generated by outbound | Existing clients asking for more work |
| Calls booked | First calls on the calendar | Reschedules counted twice, internal meetings |
| Calls held | First calls that actually happened | Anything under five minutes |
| Fit | Held calls that match the ICP | See the ICP rule below |
| Opportunities | Fit calls with a real next step | Anything with no date |
| Signed | Work agreed, by segment | Verbal with no signature |
| Revenue | Recognised revenue, split by segment | One-off pass-through costs |

## One source per metric

Two systems will always disagree. Pick the one closer to the event and write the choice down.

| Metric | Choose |
|---|---|
| Channel attribution | The activity tracker where the work was logged when it happened, not the CRM's guess afterwards |
| Calls booked | The calendar |
| Calls held | The sales tracker, filled in by whoever ran the call |
| Fit | The CRM |
| Revenue | The billing system |

Booked and held come from different places on purpose. Taking both from one source makes the no-show rate unmeasurable, because one system never records the absence.

## The ICP rule

A held call with no deal record created counts as not a fit.

Harsh, and deliberately so. If the person who ran the call did not think it worth creating a record, it was not a fit, whatever anyone says at the end of the month. It also makes the number self-maintaining: nobody has to grade fit retrospectively.

## The segment split

Split signed work and revenue into the current motion and everything that predates it. Two lines, every week.

Without the split, an existing client base masks whatever the new motion is doing for about a year, which is long enough to keep funding something that is not working or to kill something that is.

## No-shows

```
no-shows = calls booked (calendar) - calls held (sales tracker)
```

Weekly, not monthly, because the fix is operational: reminder timing, how far out calls are booked, who sends the confirmation. Monthly numbers are too coarse to connect to a change.

A rate above a quarter is a booking problem, not a demand problem, and no amount of extra top-of-funnel fixes it.

## Per sender

Where more than one person does outbound, record activity by person as well as by channel. Channel totals hide the fact that one person's messages work and another's do not, and that is the actionable finding.

## Changing a definition

Sometimes necessary. When it happens, write the change and the date into the history, and never retrofit earlier weeks. Two honest series with a marked break beat one series that quietly means two different things.
