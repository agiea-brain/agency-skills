# Scoring a path

Strength is about the relationship, not the connector's title. A tier 4 connection to a manager is worth more than a tier 1 connection to a CEO, because the ask only works if the person wants to make it.

## The tiers

| Tier | What it means | What can be asked |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Worked together closely. They would take your call today. | A direct introduction, with their endorsement |
| 3 | Worked together, or known for years, contact is occasional | An introduction, if you make it easy |
| 2 | Met a few times, friendly, no shared work | Permission to mention their name, not an intro |
| 1 | Connected online, never really spoken | Nothing. This is a cold contact wearing a warm label. |

Tier 1 is where most intro requests go to die. Treat it as cold and write to the target directly instead.

## Modifiers

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Has referred you work before | Up one tier, whatever else is true |
| You have referred them work | Up one tier |
| Last contact over 18 months | Down one tier until re-established |
| You have never given them anything | Cap at tier 3, and fix that before asking |
| They have declined an ask before | Down one tier, and change what you ask for |

The "never given them anything" cap is the one worth enforcing. A network worked only in one direction stops producing within a year.

## Ranking the list

Sort by tier, then by how well the target fits the ICP. A tier 4 path to a marginal account outranks a tier 2 path to a perfect one, because the tier 4 ask will actually happen and the tier 2 ask probably will not.

Work five to eight a week. More than that and the asks get templated, which the connectors notice immediately.

## Two hops

One hop is someone who knows the target. Two is someone who knows someone who does, and it only works when the first hop is tier 4 and knows exactly why the introduction matters.

Three hops is a cold email with extra steps and a worse hit rate, because each handoff loses the reason. Do not build them.

## Recording outcomes

Four results per ask: intro made, intro promised and not made, declined, no response.

The middle two are the useful ones. An intro promised and never made usually means the connector could not find a way to phrase it, which is a problem with the ask, not with them. Rewrite and try the pattern on the next one.

No response twice from the same person means the relationship is a tier lower than scored. Adjust it in the inventory rather than asking a third time.
