# The ask

The connector should have to do almost nothing. Two sentences to you, one forward, done.

## Shape

```
[Why you're asking them specifically, one line.]
[What you want, named target and named reason.]
[A forwardable paragraph they can send as-is.]
[An explicit out.]
```

## Example

> Hey Dana, you were at Northwind when we rebuilt their quoting flow, so you've seen how we work.
>
> I'm trying to reach Priya Shah at Harbour Logistics. They're going through the same mess Northwind was in 2024 and I think we could help.
>
> If you're up for it, here's something you could forward:
>
> *"Priya, meet Bailey. His team rebuilt our quoting flow at Northwind and cut turnaround from five days to a few hours. Given what you said about your intake backlog, worth twenty minutes."*
>
> And if it's not a connection you want to make, no problem at all, just say so and I'll leave it.

Four parts: why them, what you want, the forwardable paragraph, the out.

## The forwardable paragraph

The part people skip, and the part that decides whether the intro happens. A connector who has to compose the introduction themselves will put it off, then feel bad, then avoid you.

Write it in their voice, not yours. Third person, short, with one concrete result in it. Three sentences at most.

## The out

Always included, always explicit. Two reasons: it makes saying no cheap, which protects the relationship, and it raises the yes rate, because an ask with no escape reads as pressure.

"No problem at all if you'd rather not" costs nothing and changes how the whole message lands.

## What kills an ask

- **"Any introductions you think make sense?"** Transfers all the work. The most common intro request and the least effective.
- **No named target.** Same problem, dressed up.
- **No reason.** "I'd love an intro to Priya" gives the connector nothing to say in the forward.
- **A pitch attached.** The ask is for an introduction, not a chance to sell the connector.
- **Asking a dormant contact cold.** Re-establish first, in a message with no ask in it at all, then ask weeks later.
- **Asking a current client mid-wobble.** Wait until the work is visibly going well. An intro request lands very differently when they are unhappy.

## After the intro

Reply within a day, cc the connector on the first message, then drop them from the thread so they are not copied on a negotiation.

Tell them what happened either way. The connectors who hear back are the ones who introduce you again, and that follow-up is the cheapest relationship maintenance available.

## Giving first

The inventory caps anyone you have never helped at tier 3 for a reason. Before a quarter of asks, make a handful of introductions for other people, with no expectation attached. Networks that only ever receive stop producing, and it takes about a year to notice.
