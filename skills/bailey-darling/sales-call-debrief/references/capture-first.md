# Capture before you judge

Two separate acts, in this order. Assessing while listening is how a call gets remembered as better than it was, and how the prospect's own words get replaced by the firm's vocabulary.

## What to capture, verbatim

Three to five lines, in their words, with no interpretation.

| Capture | Why it is the one that matters |
|---|---|
| How they described the problem | Their phrasing is what makes the follow-up land, and it is the phrasing their colleagues will recognise too |
| Any number they volunteered | Budget, headcount, hours, how long it has dragged on. Unprompted numbers are real; prompted ones are estimates. |
| What they have already tried | The objection the whole market carries, said out loud by one person |
| What they said about timing | Especially the trigger. "Before the new VP starts" is a date; "soon" is not. |
| Who else they mentioned | Every name is a stakeholder, a blocker, or a budget holder |
| The question they asked twice | They did not get a satisfying answer the first time |

## Verbatim means verbatim

> Theirs: "Every proposal starts from scratch and it's eating my Sundays."
>
> Paraphrased: "Proposal process is inefficient."

The second is accurate and useless. It cannot be quoted back, it does not sound like them, and it loses the detail, Sundays, that reveals how much it costs them personally.

Keep the quotes even when they are unflattering to the firm, and especially when they contradict what the firm assumes about its market.

## Capture the shape of the call too

Three quick observations, written before any judgment:

- **Who talked.** If the firm talked more than half, this was a pitch, not a discovery call, and the qualification that follows is guesswork.
- **Where they leaned in.** The topic that produced the most from them is the one the proposal should lead with.
- **Where they went flat.** Equally informative and usually ignored.

## Ten minutes, and why

The specific phrasing decays fast. Within an hour, the memory is of the gist; by the next day it is of the firm's own interpretation of the gist.

Ten minutes also fits between calls, which is the only reason it actually happens. A fifteen-minute process gets skipped on a busy day, and skipped debriefs are the reason pipelines rot.

## If the call was recorded

Still debrief from memory first, then check the transcript. The recording will always be there; the impression will not, and the impression is where "they went flat when I mentioned the timeline" comes from.

Pulling quotes from a transcript without a memory pass produces accurate notes about the wrong moments.
