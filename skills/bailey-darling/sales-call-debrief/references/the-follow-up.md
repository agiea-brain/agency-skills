# The follow-up

Short, in their words, sent the same day.

## Shape

```
[Their problem, in their phrasing, one line.]
[What we'd do about it, one or two lines. No capability list.]
[The next step, with a date, exactly as agreed on the call.]
```

Three or four sentences. Anything longer is the firm talking to itself.

## Example

> Sam, you said every proposal starts from scratch and it's eating your Sundays.
>
> The piece we'd start with is the scoping template and the pricing logic behind it, so the first draft writes itself and you're editing rather than starting over.
>
> You mentioned wanting this sorted before the new hire starts in January. I've put 20 minutes in for Thursday with Priya so we can work out what that looks like.

The first line is theirs, almost verbatim. That is what makes it read as a reply to a conversation rather than a template.

## Rules

**Quote them.** One line of their phrasing, lightly cleaned up. It is the whole reason capture comes first.

**No capability list.** They were on the call; they know what the firm does. A follow-up that restates the offering reads as a brochure and signals that nothing was heard.

**One next step, dated.** The step agreed on the call, not a more ambitious one. Upgrading the next step in the follow-up is the fastest way to stall a deal that was moving.

**Nothing attached, usually.** A deck or a case study attached to a first follow-up moves the conversation from their problem to the firm's evidence, too early. Send it when they ask, which they will if the problem was named properly.

**Same day.** A next-day follow-up lands in a different context and reads as routine.

## When there was no agreed next step

Do not invent one. Ask instead.

> "We didn't land on a next step. Is this worth picking up after the quarter close, or is it not the right time?"

Direct, easy to answer either way, and it produces a real answer far more often than a proposed meeting does. A "not right now" today is worth more than three months of polite chasing.

## When the signer was not in the room

Ask for them, specifically, and give the person a reason that helps them internally.

> "Who else would need to be comfortable with this? Happy to do a short version of today's conversation for them, so you're not the one having to re-explain it."

The second sentence matters: it offers to save them work rather than asking them to do some.

## Logging it

The follow-up goes in the record with the verbatim lines and the category. Three weeks later, the record should let anyone reconstruct what the prospect wanted and in whose words, without opening an inbox.
