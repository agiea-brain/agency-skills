# Qualifying, honestly

Same five checks every call. The consistency is what makes the pipeline comparable; the honesty is what makes it useful.

## The five

| Check | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Fit** | They look like clients the firm has done well for | "We could probably help them" |
| **Problem** | They named something specific, in their words, with a cost | They expressed interest in the category |
| **Authority** | The person who signs was in the room, or is named and reachable | "I'll take it to the team" with no names |
| **Timing** | A date or a trigger event exists | "Sometime this year", "when things settle" |
| **Next step** | A specific thing, on a date, that both sides agreed | "I'll follow up next week" |

A next step only counts when it is in both calendars or has a date attached that the prospect said out loud. The firm deciding to follow up is not a next step; it is an intention.

## The three categories

**Real.** Problem named, authority identified, a date exists. Work it.

**Maybe.** Problem named, missing authority or timing. The next action is to get the missing piece, not to send a proposal. Most of a healthy pipeline lives here and that is fine, as long as everyone knows which piece is missing.

**Not.** No problem named, or no fit. Say so in the record and stop. Interest is not a problem, and a pipeline full of interest is how a quarter gets lost.

Most firms have no "not" category, which is why their pipeline is three times its real size and their forecast is fiction.

## Interest is not a buying signal

The single most common qualification error. A prospect who is engaged, asks good questions, says "this is really interesting" and names no problem of their own is not a prospect; they are an interested person.

The test: can you write one sentence describing what is going wrong for them right now, in their words, with a cost attached? If not, the call produced enthusiasm and nothing else.

## Authority without the authority in the room

Common and recoverable, but only if the next step is access rather than a proposal.

Sending a proposal to someone who cannot sign means it gets forwarded without its context, read by someone who did not hear the conversation, and judged on price. The next step is a second call with the signer, and the follow-up should ask for it directly.

## Timing and triggers

A date is good. A trigger is better, because it is the thing that will actually make them act: a contract ending, a person starting or leaving, a launch, a funding round, a season.

Record the trigger even when it is months out. A pipeline of dated triggers is workable; a pipeline of "Q2" is not.

## Being wrong

Review categories against outcomes once a quarter. Two patterns to look for:

- Deals categorised Real that never closed. Usually authority was assumed rather than confirmed.
- Deals categorised Not or Maybe that closed anyway. Usually the problem was named and not recognised as one.

The second pattern is more useful, and it generally means the firm's idea of its own ICP is narrower than reality.
