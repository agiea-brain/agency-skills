# Sorting the list

## The real reason, not the recorded one

Most records say "budget" because that is what people say when they leave. Write down what actually happened, from memory and from the last few conversations.

| Recorded | Often actually |
|---|---|
| Budget | The work was not producing enough to defend |
| Going in another direction | A specific thing went wrong |
| Pausing for now | Soft no, and they hoped nobody would follow up |
| Champion left | The engagement never had anyone else behind it |
| Taking it in house | Someone got hired and needed to justify the role |

Being honest here is the whole exercise. A list sorted on the polite reason produces messages aimed at the wrong thing.

## The fields

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| `contact` and whether they are still there | Decides whether this is a win-back at all |
| `left when` | The three-to-eighteen-month window |
| `recorded reason` | What the file says |
| `real reason` | What happened |
| `how it ended` | Warm, neutral, or badly. Decides tone. |
| `what they bought` | Named specifically, for the message |
| `outcome` | Did the work produce anything they would still claim credit for |
| `who else is still there` | A second path if the first contact has gone |

## The outcome field

The one people skip and the one that decides everything. If the work produced a result they would still claim credit for internally, the win-back is easy and the message can reference it. If it did not, no change to the offer will matter, and this is a repair conversation or nothing.

## Priority order

1. **Good outcome, left on budget, contact still there.** Highest hit rate by a distance.
2. **Good outcome, champion moved to a new company.** Not a win-back, a warm new account, and usually the biggest opportunity on the list.
3. **Good outcome, scope gap.** Clean if the gap is now closed.
4. **Neutral outcome, took it in house.** Worth an honest question with no pitch attached.
5. **Good outcome, champion left, replacement never engaged.** A fresh sale into a warm company.
6. **Poor outcome, specific fixable cause.** Only with a real fix, and expect a low hit rate.
7. **Ended badly.** Repair conversation only. Not on a win-back list.
8. **Bad fit.** Not on any list.

Work one to three first. Those three groups are where almost all recovered revenue comes from, and they are usually a quarter of the list.

## Batching

Five to ten at a time, then stop and read the replies. Win-back wording is hard to get right first time, and the first batch teaches more than any amount of drafting.

A whole list sent at once is a campaign, and it is identifiable as one from the inside.

## Recording outcomes

Against the departure reason, not just per person. After two or three rounds this answers which reasons are actually recoverable at this firm, which makes the next list ten minutes of work instead of an afternoon.

Most firms find one or two categories produce nearly everything and quietly drop the rest.
