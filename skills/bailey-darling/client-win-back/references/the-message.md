# The message

Short, specific about the old work, honest about the gap, and easy to decline.

## Shape

```
[Name the gap. One clause, no apology, no explanation of your absence.]
[Name the specific work you did for them.]
[What changed, in one sentence.]
[A small ask, with an explicit out.]
```

## By departure reason

**Left on budget**

> Sam, it's been about a year. We rebuilt your quoting flow back in 2024 and I still point to it.
>
> The reason I'm writing: we've started doing smaller phased engagements, which is closer to the shape that would have worked for you at the time.
>
> Worth a short call, or is it not a priority right now? Either answer is fine.

**Scope gap**

> Dana, we did the brand work for you in early 2025 and then you had to go elsewhere for the build. We do that part now, properly rather than as an add-on. If it's still being handled somewhere you're not happy with, happy to talk. If it's sorted, ignore me.

**Took it in house**

> Mark, you brought this in house about eighteen months ago. Genuinely curious how it's gone, no pitch attached. Most of the teams I've seen do it find the first year is fine and the second gets harder. If that's where you are, I'd be glad to compare notes.

**Champion moved to a new company**

> Priya, congratulations on the move. We worked together on the quoting project at Northwind, which I still think is the best version of that work we've done. No agenda, but if you're looking at anything similar in the new role, I'd love to hear about it.

Note this one leads with them, not with the old engagement. A new company is a new sale with a warm path, not a win-back.

**Something went wrong, and it is fixed**

> Jay, I've thought about how the launch went for us more than once. The handover was the part we got wrong, and we've changed how we do it, specifically [the change]. Not asking for anything, but I wanted you to know it wasn't lost on us.

That last one carries no ask at all, deliberately. If there is a way back, it starts with them replying to this, not with a meeting request.

## Rules

**Name the gap in the first line.** Writing as though no time has passed is the most common error, and it reads as either careless or calculated.

**No apology for the silence.** "Sorry I've been out of touch" makes it about the sender and asks them to absolve you. One clause acknowledging the time is enough.

**Name the specific work.** "We worked together" is weak. "We rebuilt your quoting flow" proves it was real and reminds them of a result.

**Make no easy.** "Or is it not a priority right now" gets a real answer. An ask with no out gets silence, and silence tells you nothing.

**Nothing attached.** No deck, no case study, no calendar link in a first message.

**One follow-up, then stop.** Two weeks later, two lines. After that the answer is no, and continuing costs the relationship the next change might need.

## What never goes in

- A list of new services
- "We've grown a lot since then"
- Any framing that implies they made a mistake by leaving
- A discount as the opening move, which prices the relationship at the discount
- A calendar link in the first message
