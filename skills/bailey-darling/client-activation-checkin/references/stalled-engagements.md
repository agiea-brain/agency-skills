# When it stalls

An engagement sitting at Built with no movement for three or more check-ins. Common, rarely named, and the single largest source of quiet churn.

## Recognising it

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Same blocker three check-ins running | Not a blocker, a priority |
| The sponsor stops joining, sends someone junior | Attention has moved |
| Calls get rescheduled twice | Same |
| Questions stop | Nobody is using it, so nobody has questions |
| "We've just been slammed" twice | Honest and final, unless something changes |
| Payment still prompt, engagement still dead | The most deceptive case. Money is not attention. |

That last one catches people out. A client who pays on time and does nothing feels fine on a dashboard and will not renew.

## The conversation

Have it directly, once, at the third stalled check-in. Not another agenda, a different call.

> "We've had the same blocker for three calls now. That usually means something changed on your side, which is completely fine, but I'd rather know than keep booking these. Is this still a priority this quarter?"

Three things that makes possible. They say yes and commit properly. They say no and you stop burning time. Or they say what the real obstacle is, which is usually something they were embarrassed to raise.

All three outcomes are better than a fourth check-in.

## If they say not now

Agree an explicit pause with a date to revisit, and stop the check-ins. A standing call nobody wants is worse for the relationship than an honest pause.

Record what would need to be true for it to restart. That sentence is what makes the restart conversation easy in two months.

## If the obstacle is internal to them

Usually one of three: no owner assigned, a decision nobody will make, or someone quietly against it.

The firm can help with the first and sometimes the second. The third needs the sponsor, and the most useful thing to ask is who else needs to be comfortable with this.

## Preventing the stall

Most stalls are set up at kickoff, not discovered later.

- Name the internal owner at kickoff, not the sponsor. Different person, usually.
- Define the milestone as a thing the client's own people do, not a thing the firm delivers.
- Agree what happens after handover before the handover.
- Put the first in-use date on the calendar at kickoff, with a name against it.

Those four take ten minutes at the start and prevent most of what this page is about.
