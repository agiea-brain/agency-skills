# The prep card and the agenda

Built in ten minutes before the call. Internal, except the agenda, which the client sees in advance.

## The card

```
[CLIENT] - check-in [n] - [date]

MILESTONE: [one sentence, their words, unchanged since last time]
STAGE: Built, 5 weeks | In use

WHERE THEY ARE
[One sentence on what is actually true today.]

THE GAP
[One sentence: what stands between today and the milestone.]

BLOCKER: [what] | OWNED BY: [firm / client / third party]
[If client-owned and unacknowledged: RAISE IN FIRST 10 MINUTES]

SINCE LAST TIME
[What they said or did in the shared channel. Or: nothing, 3 weeks.]

LAST TIME'S COMMITMENTS
- Us: [what] - done / not
- Them: [what] - done / not

OPENER
[One question referencing something specific from since last time.]
```

## Reading the shared channel

Before opening any record, read the working channel with the client. Five minutes, looking for:

- Questions they asked, especially ones a real user would ask
- Something they went quiet on after being enthusiastic
- A new name appearing, which usually means a new stakeholder
- Complaints, however mild
- The last thing they said, and how long ago

Silence is the most informative signal here. A client who was active and has said nothing for three weeks has deprioritised, and the opener should be about that rather than about the milestone.

## The agenda, sent in advance

Three lines, the day before.

```
Thursday's call, keeping it to 20 minutes:
1. Where we are on [milestone, their words]
2. [The blocker], and what we each need to do about it
3. What's next after this lands
```

Short, specific, and it names the blocker before the call so nobody is surprised into defensiveness.

Sent in advance it changes who prepares. A client who has read it arrives having thought about the blocker, which is the entire purpose.

## The opener

References something specific since last time. "How's it going" gets "good, we've been slammed", which is a non-answer everybody has agreed to accept.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "How's everything going?" | "You mentioned Priya's team would start using it after the quarter close. Did that happen?" |
| "Any updates?" | "I noticed the channel's been quiet for a few weeks. Has something else taken priority?" |
| "Are you happy with the work?" | "Has anyone outside your team touched it yet?" |

## The close

One commitment from each side, with a name and a date, said out loud and written into the record before the call ends.

The client-side commitment is the one that matters. An engagement where only the firm ever owes something is an engagement the client has stopped participating in, and that shows up at renewal as a surprise.

## When to cancel instead

If the card has no gap and no blocker, there is nothing to move. Send two lines in the channel instead and give everyone the half hour back.

Cancelling a check-in that has no purpose builds more credibility than holding it, and it makes the ones that do happen matter.
