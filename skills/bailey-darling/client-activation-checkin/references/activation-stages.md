# Delivered is not in use

Two stages, tracked separately. Most firms track the first and report it as if it were the second.

| Stage | What it means | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Built** | The work exists and the client has approved it | Signed off, invoiced, sitting there |
| **In use** | The people it was built for are using it | Usage, questions, complaints, requests |

A client can sit at Built for months. Everything feels fine, invoices are paid, and at renewal they cannot name what it did for them, because nothing happened.

Mark the stage explicitly on every engagement and report the second one. The first is a milestone for the firm; the second is the only one that matters to the client.

## Who "the people it was built for" are

Depends on the work, and naming them specifically is most of the job:

| The work | In use means |
|---|---|
| A website or campaign | Live, with traffic, and the client's team updating it |
| A brand or design system | Other people inside the client making things with it, without asking |
| A process or playbook | Their team following it without the firm in the room |
| Something client-facing | Their own clients using it, not just their internal team |
| A report or dashboard | Someone opening it in a meeting and making a decision from it |

The last column is the milestone. Write it in those words.

## Telling which stage a client is in

Three questions, answerable from the shared channel without asking the client:

1. Has anyone outside the immediate project team touched it?
2. Has anyone asked a question that only a real user would ask?
3. Has anything been changed or requested since handover?

Three noes means Built, whatever the status field says. One yes means it is beginning. Three yeses means In use, and the engagement has done its job.

## The gap between the stages

This is where engagements quietly die, and it is almost never the firm's delivery that causes it. Usual causes, in order:

- **No internal owner at the client.** The sponsor bought it, nobody was assigned to run it.
- **It needs one more thing nobody scoped.** A migration, an access grant, a piece of content, a decision.
- **The people it was built for were never told.** Common and easy to fix.
- **Their priority changed.** Honest, and worth knowing early.
- **Nobody is confident enough to use it.** A training problem the firm can solve cheaply, if it hears about it.

Each has a different fix and only the last two need the client to do anything hard, which is why diagnosing before the call matters.

## Measuring it

Per client, one line: current stage, the date it reached that stage, and how long it has been there.

Time at Built is the number to watch. Past a few weeks with no movement, the check-in stops being a check-in and becomes a conversation about whether this is still happening.
