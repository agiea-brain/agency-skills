# Pain math: what to capture and how to convert it

Collect during the vertical drill. Convert during the magic wand block. Never convert out loud during the drill; it turns an interview into a negotiation.

## Capture sheet

| Field | Question that gets it | Example answer |
|---|---|---|
| The activity | "Walk me through the process start to finish." | Monthly client reporting |
| The biggest step | "Which step eats the most time?" | Pulling data from four sources into the deck |
| Share of the work | "Is that 80%? 50%? Rough." | About 70% |
| Frequency | "How often? Per client? Per week?" | Once a month per client, 12 clients |
| Hours per occurrence | Usually volunteered with the step; ask if not | 3 hours each |
| Who does it | "Who's doing it?" | An account manager |
| Loaded cost | "What's their loaded cost roughly?" | $90 an hour |
| Already tried | "Have you tried to solve this? What did you do?" | A reporting tool, abandoned after two months |
| Why it failed | "Why didn't that work?" | Still needed manual cleanup every time |

## The two numbers you need

**Annual cost of the pain**

hours per occurrence × occurrences per year × loaded hourly cost

Example: 3 hours × (12 clients × 12 months) × $90 = $38,880 a year.

Use the owner's figures even when they look rough. The number only has to be theirs.

**Opportunity cost**

What they said they would do with the time, in dollars. Prompt with "what's a new client worth to you per month" or "what would a hire unlock". A new client at $4,000 a month is $48,000 a year of upside they are not pursuing.

If they go small on the opportunity, agree that it is huge and ask whether it translates to more work or to headspace for something else. Either answer gives you a second number.

## The sentence you should be able to say by minute 22

"[Owner] is losing about [annual cost] a year on [the biggest step], which is stopping them from [opportunity], and they said it matters because [their words]."

If any bracket is empty, go back to the block that fills it. Do not move to pricing without all four.

## Common misses

- Counting the whole activity instead of the biggest step. The build will target the step; price against the step.
- Taking "it's annoying" as the value. Annoying is not a number. Ask what it costs or what it blocks.
- Accepting the owner's own time at zero. Ask what their hour is worth to a client, or what they bill.
- Letting a second pain into the sheet. Write it in the margin for the follow-up and go back to the first one.
