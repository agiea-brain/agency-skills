# The report

One screen. The client reads the first three lines on a phone and knows how the week went. Everything else is there for the person who wants it.

```
[CLIENT] - week of [date range]

THE WEEK
[One line: good, bad or flat, and the single reason.]

                  This week    vs last    vs 4-wk avg
Sessions              2,410       +12%          +4%
Conversions              38        -5%          -1%
Conversion rate        1.6%       -15%          -5%
Spend                $4,200         0%          +2%
Cost per conv          $141       +18%          +9%

WHAT MOVED
[Only flagged items. Each one: what moved, then why, then the evidence.]
- Cost per conversion up 18% on flat spend. Frequency hit 2.9 on the
  prospecting set, CTR down 24%. Creative fatigue, not auction pressure.

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. [Paid] Swap the three prospecting creatives for the new set.
   Expect cost per conversion back near $120 within 10 days. Us, Tuesday.
2. [Site] Pricing page is 41% of entries and converts at 0.7%.
   Test the form above the fold. Us, next sprint.

LAST WEEK'S RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Raise search budget 20% - done Monday, impression share lost to
   budget fell from 31% to 9%. Working.
2. Fix the mobile nav - not started. Carried.

Primary conversion: demo request. Attribution: [model]. Paid: [platforms].
```

## Rules of the format

**The week** is one line and it commits. "Flat, with paid costs drifting up" is a judgment. "Mixed results across channels" is not.

**The table** is five rows and three columns. Always both comparison columns. No colour coding: red and green do the thinking for the reader and they do it badly, because a spend increase is not inherently bad.

**What moved** only lists flagged items. Each entry is what, then why, then the evidence that rules out the alternatives. If the why is not known yet, write that and say what will be checked. An honest unknown beats a confident guess that gets quoted back in three weeks.

**Recommendations** are one per channel, maximum three. Each one: action, expected effect, owner, date. A recommendation with no owner is an observation.

**Last week's recommendations** carry forward with their outcome: done and it worked, done and it did not, or not started. This section is what makes the report compound, and it is the first section people drop when busy.

**The footer** names the primary conversion, the attribution model and the platforms. It settles the quarterly argument about why these numbers do not match the ones in the ad platform.

## Length

If it does not fit on one screen, cut the commentary, not the recommendations.

## Delivery

The same day, the same time, every week. A report that lands late is read later, and a report read later is not acted on. Delivering a thin report on time beats a thorough one on Thursday.
