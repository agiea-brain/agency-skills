# Scoring and ranking

## The gate

A cluster is not a topic until three different companies have raised it. Not three mentions, three companies. One prospect who asks the same thing five times is one data point and a sign they were not given a clear answer.

Below the gate, clusters go in a holding file and are re-checked next quarter. A two-company cluster that reaches four next quarter is a trend; the same cluster still at two is a coincidence.

## Four factors

| Factor | Scale | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | companies raising it, 3+ | Raw count, not normalised |
| Cycle point | `pre` / `discovery` = 3, `evaluation` = 2, `proposal` = 2, `delivery` = 3, `renewal` = 1 | Both ends of the cycle score high, the middle less |
| Cost | 3 if it is on record as a reason a deal was lost or stalled, 1 if raised and resolved, 0 otherwise | Needs a lost-deal note to support it |
| Coverage gap | 3 if nothing exists, 2 if something exists but is thin or buried, 0 if well covered | Check the site before scoring |

Score is reach plus the three weights. Rank on it, then read the top of the list with judgment rather than taking the order literally. The scoring is there to stop the loudest recent call from leading the plan, not to replace a decision.

## Why delivery scores as high as discovery

Discovery questions are what every competitor also hears and writes about. Delivery questions are what only a firm that has actually done the work hears, which makes them the hardest content for a competitor to copy and the most convincing proof that the firm has done this before.

Most content plans ignore them entirely, because the person writing content is not on delivery calls.

## Red flags in the scored list

- **Everything scores within two points.** The gate is too loose or the clusters are too broad. Split them.
- **Top five are all `proposal`.** This is a sales-enablement backlog, not a content plan. Deliver it as such.
- **Top topic has reach 3 and nothing else.** Barely cleared the gate. Rank it below a reach-8 topic even if the formula disagrees.
- **A topic scores high on coverage gap and has been on the list before.** It was planned and never written. That is a capacity problem, and adding it again changes nothing.

## Quarter-over-quarter

Three movements worth naming when the plan is presented:

| Movement | Reading |
|---|---|
| Topic drops off after an asset shipped | The asset worked. Say so; it is the only evidence content marketing ever produces. |
| Topic persists after an asset shipped | Nobody can find it, or sales is not sending it. Distribution problem, not a content one. |
| New topic enters at high reach | Something changed in the market or in the firm's own positioning. Worth understanding before writing. |
