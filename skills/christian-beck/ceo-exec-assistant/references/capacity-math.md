# Capacity

Done before the task list is read, because a list read without this always looks achievable.

## The arithmetic

```
working hours          = days in the week x hours per day      (default 5 x 8 = 40)
committed hours        = sum of accepted calendar events, excluding all-day and tentative
admin allowance        = 25% of working hours                   (default 10)
available hours        = working - committed - admin
```

Admin covers email, Slack, invoicing, the unplanned client call, the thing that breaks. Twenty-five percent is the default for someone running a small agency and also carrying client work. Measure it once over a month and replace the default; most people find theirs is higher than they expected, not lower.

## Reading the result

| Available hours | Reading |
|---|---|
| Over 20 | A real working week. Ambitious items belong here. |
| 12 to 20 | Normal. Three priorities, nothing more. |
| 6 to 12 | A meeting week. Priorities are small and finishable, or they will not happen. |
| Under 6 | No deep work is possible. Say so in the brief's first line and move everything that needs focus. |

The under-6 case is the one that matters. Those weeks happen, and the damage comes from pretending otherwise and ending the week with five things half done.

## Per day, not just per week

A week with 18 available hours split as 0, 0, 2, 8, 8 is a different week from one split 4, 4, 3, 4, 3. Run the same subtraction per day and put the biggest item against the biggest block.

| Meeting hours in a day | What fits |
|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | One deep item. Protect it. |
| 2 to 3 | One medium item plus small ones |
| 4+ | Small items only. No deep work. |
| 6+ | Admin and recovery. Schedule nothing. |

## What counts as committed

Counted: accepted meetings, client calls, anything with another human in it, travel time around external meetings.

Not counted: all-day events, tentative invitations, self-created focus blocks. A focus block is capacity being spent, not capacity being lost, and counting it twice makes the week look fuller than it is.

Travel gets counted and almost never is. An external meeting across town is a half day.

## Prep time

Any client presentation, pitch or review needs prep booked as a calendar block before the week starts. The default is one hour of prep per hour of client-facing presentation, doubled for a first pitch to a new client.

If that block is not on the calendar, the brief's first finding is that it is missing. Prep that lives in the gaps is prep that happens at 11pm the night before.
