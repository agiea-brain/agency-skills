# Triage

Four buckets, applied in order. A task lands in the first bucket it qualifies for and appears once.

## 1. Due today

Due date is today or earlier and not blocked. These are the only items that compete for today's hours.

An item overdue by more than seven days does not belong here. It goes to the decision list at the bottom of this page.

## 2. Due this week

Due inside the next seven days. Sorted by due date, then by whether anyone else is blocked by it. Work that unblocks another person outranks work that only affects its owner, every time, because the second kind only costs one person's time.

## 3. At risk

Any one of these makes an item at risk:

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| Due in under 3 days with no work logged | Nothing has started |
| Estimated hours exceed remaining available hours before the due date | Arithmetic, not opinion |
| Blocked by someone who has not responded in 3 working days | The blocker is the task now |
| Due date moved twice | The date is not real |
| Belongs to a client presentation this week with no prep block booked | Prep that is not scheduled does not happen |
| Approval task sitting more than 2 days | Someone downstream is idle |

That last one is the most expensive and the least visible. A person running an agency is usually the approver, and an approval sitting in their queue stops somebody else's week, not their own.

## 4. Waiting on someone else

Not theirs to do. Listed with who owes what and how long it has been. It exists so the brief can produce one line of chasing rather than a week of wondering.

Over five working days with no response, it stops being a wait and becomes a decision: chase, escalate, or proceed without them.

## The decision list

Items that are not really tasks any more go here, with a recommendation each.

- Overdue by more than a week: do it this week, move it with a real date, or delete it. Three options, pick one.
- In the at-risk bucket three weeks running: it has been declined by behaviour. Say so.
- No due date and untouched for a month: it is a note, not a task. Move it out of the task list.

Working this list takes five minutes and is the only thing that stops the task list growing until nobody trusts it.

## What the brief never does on its own

Change a due date, reassign, close, delete, or create a calendar block. It proposes; the owner decides. Anything touching more than one item is confirmed first, because a bulk reschedule that was not asked for is indistinguishable from losing the week's commitments.
