# The brief

Read in two minutes, standing up, before the first meeting.

```
WEEK OF [date]

18 available hours. 14 committed to meetings, 8 for admin.
Wednesday is your only clear day.

DO FIRST
1. Finish the Harbour proposal. 4h. Wednesday morning.
2. Approve TJ's brand concepts. 30m. Today, he is blocked.
3. Send the Northwind scope revision. 1h. Thursday.

NOT HAPPENING THIS WEEK
- Website audit for Illume. Needs 6h, you have 3 after the above.
  Moving to the 11th unless you want to drop something else.

AT RISK
- Audaz deck. Presentation Thursday 2pm, no prep booked. Needs 2h.
- Proximity invoice approval. Sitting 4 days, accounts are waiting.

WAITING ON
- Sara, pricing sign-off, 2 days
- Illume, feedback on round two, 6 days. Past chasing, needs a decision.

DECIDE
- "Rework onboarding doc" overdue 23 days. Do it, date it, or delete it.
```

## Rules of the format

**The capacity line comes first**, before any task. It frames everything under it. Three numbers and the one sentence about where the clear time is.

**Do first** is three items, ordered, each with an estimate and a day. Each one finishable. If an item cannot be finished in the time available, it is the wrong item and the right one is a piece of it.

**Not happening** is the section that earns the brief. Name the item, why it does not fit, and where it goes. Never silently omit it: a dropped item that was never mentioned comes back as a surprise on Friday.

**At risk** carries the reason and the remedy, not just the flag. "No prep booked, needs 2h" is actionable. "At risk" alone is not.

**Waiting on** is one line each: who, what, how long. Anything over five days is marked as needing a decision rather than another chase.

**Decide** is the overdue and the stale, with the three options spelled out. Usually one or two items. If it is more than five, the task list needs a clear-out rather than a brief.

## Tone

Flat and specific. No encouragement, no "you've got this", no summary of how busy the week looks. The person reading it already knows they are busy; what they need is the arithmetic and the cut.

## Cadence

Monday morning, before the first meeting. Optionally a three-line version each morning: today's available hours, today's one thing, anything that became at risk overnight.

Never more than that. A daily brief that takes five minutes to read is a meeting with yourself.
