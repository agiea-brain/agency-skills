# Where the signals are

Ranked by how often they turn into signed work. The order is the opposite of where most firms look.

## 1. Unbilled work already delivered

Everything done this quarter that was not in scope. The extra round, the deck that was not quoted, the "quick favour" that took two days, the person cc'd on everything who is not on the contract.

Converts best because there is nothing to prove. The client already receives it and already values it. The conversation is about the scope catching up with reality, not about buying something new.

Count the hours before the meeting. "We delivered about 60 hours outside scope last quarter" is a different conversation from "we've been doing a bit extra".

## 2. What the team works around

Anything the delivery team patches repeatedly. A broken handoff, a file the client never sends on time, a system nobody owns, a step done manually every month.

The client may not have named it. The brief's job is to name it in their terms and in hours or money, not to describe the workaround.

## 3. Asked for and never quoted

Requests that came up in a call and got a "we could look at that" and nothing else. Once is a note. Twice is an opportunity. Three times with no quote is a failure of account management, and the brief should say so internally even if it does not say so to the client.

## 4. Their stated plans

Something the client said they are doing next quarter that the firm could do or support. Hiring, a launch, a funding round, a conference, entering a new segment.

Weaker than it looks, because plans slip and the client knows their plans better than you do. Good as the timing hook for an opportunity from sources 1 to 3; thin as the opportunity itself.

## 5. Usage and results data

What the work produced, and where the numbers stopped. Useful as proof inside a brief built on something above, rarely enough on its own.

## 6. The firm's service list

Last. This is the source most briefs start from, and it is the reason most upsell conversations sound like a vendor growing an account. Use it only to put a name and a price on an opportunity the first five sources already found.

## Collecting this without a research project

Fifteen minutes with the people who do the work, once a quarter, two questions:

- What did we do for them that we did not bill for?
- What do you keep having to fix or work around on this account?

Delivery teams answer both instantly. They have never been asked.
