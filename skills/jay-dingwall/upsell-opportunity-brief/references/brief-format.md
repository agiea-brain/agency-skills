# The brief

One page, internal. Read before the meeting, never sent to the client.

```
[CLIENT] - upsell brief - [date]
Current: [what they pay, for what, since when]
Health: [pass] - [one line of evidence]

THE OPPORTUNITY
[One sentence. What the firm would do and what it changes for them.]

THE EVIDENCE
- They said: "[verbatim, with who and when]"
- We delivered [X hours / N instances] of this outside scope last quarter
- The team has worked around [specific thing] on [N] occasions

THE PRICE
[Number and shape: retainer, project, rate change. One option.]

WHO SIGNS
[Name, role. Last in a room with us: [date]. Knows our work: yes/no.]

WHEN
[Date to raise it, and the meeting it belongs in.]

THE OPENING LINE
"[The one sentence that starts the conversation.]"

IF THEY SAY NO
[The one thing to learn from the no.]
```

## The opening line

Drafted before the price. Three properties:

1. It quotes or paraphrases something the client said.
2. It describes their situation, not the firm's service.
3. It can be said out loud without a deck.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "We wanted to talk about expanding our scope." | "You mentioned the team can't keep up with landing pages. We've built nine for you outside scope this quarter. Can we talk about making that part of the engagement?" |
| "We have a design system offering." | "Every new page takes us three rounds because there's no component library. Want me to price fixing that once?" |

If the sentence cannot be written, the opportunity is not understood well enough to raise.

## One option, not three

A menu transfers the decision back to the client, and a busy client declines a menu. Pick the one with the best evidence behind it and hold the others for next quarter.

The exception is price shape, not substance: the same opportunity as a project or as a retainer is one option with two ways to pay.

## If they say no

Fill this in before the meeting. The useful nos are specific: not now, not that, not at that price, not me who decides. Each points somewhere different, and deciding in advance what to listen for turns a declined pitch into account intelligence rather than a bad afternoon.

## What never goes in

- Capability descriptions. The client knows what the firm does.
- Case studies from other clients. This brief is about their evidence.
- More than one opportunity.
- Anything the delivery team has not confirmed. A brief built on an opportunity the team cannot staff is worse than no brief.
