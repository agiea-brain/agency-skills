# The gates

Four, in order. A failure at any gate stops the brief. Writing one anyway produces a conversation that costs more than the work was worth.

## Gate 1: account health

Fails if any of these is true:

| Condition | Why it stops everything |
|---|---|
| A delivery problem raised in the last 60 days and not closed out | Expansion reads as a firm that would rather sell than fix |
| A missed deadline this quarter with no reset conversation | Same |
| An invoice more than 30 days overdue | They have a reason they have not mentioned |
| Response times from the client doubling over a quarter | Disengagement. Find out why first. |
| The last three meetings were status, with no decisions | The relationship has gone transactional |

A failed health gate is not a delay, it is a different piece of work. Fix the account, then revisit next quarter.

## Gate 2: a named problem

The opportunity must answer something the client has said, in words that can be quoted back. Not inferred, not something the firm believes they need.

Passes: "Our team can't keep up with the volume of landing pages."
Fails: "They'd really benefit from a design system."

If nothing qualifies, the gate failure is the finding: the firm does not know enough about the client's current problems, and the next conversation is discovery, not a proposal.

## Gate 3: a budget holder

Name the person who signs. Then answer two things: have they been in a conversation with the firm this quarter, and do they already know what the firm delivers.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Signer engaged this quarter | Proceed |
| Signer exists but has not been engaged | The opportunity is access. Get in a room first. |
| Day-to-day contact says they will handle it internally | Help them sell it. Give them a one-pager in their language, not yours. |
| No signer identified | Stop. This is the whole problem. |

## Gate 4: timing

| Trigger | Raise it |
|---|---|
| Project ending | At least 6 weeks before the end |
| Retainer renewal | 8 weeks out |
| Free work conversion | At the next scheduled review, never ad hoc |
| Problem the client just named | Within 2 weeks, while it still hurts |
| Client in budget season | During planning, not after |

Fails if the only available moment is the renewal meeting itself. A first expansion conversation held in the renewal conversation makes the renewal harder, because it turns a decision about continuing into a negotiation about more.

## When a gate fails

Write one line on which gate and what would clear it, and put it where the next quarter's brief will find it. A failed gate is useful information about the account, and it is lost every time someone just quietly decides not to pitch.
