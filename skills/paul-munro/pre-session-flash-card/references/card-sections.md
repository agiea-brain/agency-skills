# Card sections

The card has a fixed order. Keep it every time. Rename a section only when the session lacks it, and add no sections.

## The standard order

1. **Why this hour matters.** One paragraph: why today, what freezes, what is at stake.
2. **Decide these before you dial.** Your own calls, made before the session starts. Anything invented for this card is tagged PROPOSED and appears here in plain language: what it is, the recommendation, adopt or kill. Once you have ruled, the line states the ruling and its date, and the PROPOSED label comes off.
3. **First five minutes.** The opening move, with the words to say in quotes.
4. **What the client decides today.** Numbered. One line of decision, one line of why. Anything that must leave the session decided has its own numbered line here. A rider attached to another decision becomes its own line or comes off the card. Nothing mandatory sits below this block.
5. **The block the client asked for.** The development or coaching work they want from the hour. This is the protected block. Its place in the run sheet is what protects it.
6. **Dates and owners.** Overdue and unset, including your own.
7. **Run sheet.** Clock time, minutes in parentheses, label, one line each. It holds one labelled slot for the client's items of at least 20 minutes.
8. **Do not reopen.** A short list of what is already settled, so nothing gets argued again.
9. **Closing posture line.** One line, set apart at the bottom: how to be in the room.

## The client-led order

Use this only once you and the client have agreed that they set the agenda, and only when they have sent their agenda note: what is decided, what is stuck, and what the hour is for.

1. Why this hour matters.
2. Decide these before you dial.
3. The client's note, in their words. Three lines, quoted or closely paraphrased, and labelled as theirs.
4. Reactions. One line per item in their note.
5. The one thing they are avoiding. Your single item, capped at ten minutes, with the words to say.
6. Dates and owners.
7. Do not reopen.
8. Closing posture line.

This order has no run sheet and no "decides today" block, because the agenda is theirs. If the note did not come, the card says so in its header and goes back to the standard order. Log the missed note against the engagement.

## The pre-read

The card is the one required piece of prep. A pre-read is an optional companion, never required reading. Build the card first, then the pre-read from the same material. Aim for about 1,000 words.

It opens with one paragraph on what changed since the last card. Then it holds what the card cannot:

- Each PROPOSED item in full: what the thing is, what stands today, what is wrong with it, what is proposed, why, what it costs, and how to stop it.
- The chain of reasoning behind each numbered decision.
- The pre-mortem: how the session or the plan could fail.
- What could not be checked.

The card carries each PROPOSED item in plain words and points to nothing else. You must be able to run the hour from the card alone. When you rule, update both files in place. The card drops the PROPOSED labels. The pre-read keeps the full block as the record, with each item retagged DECIDED and dated.

## After the session

Record the card's result in your debrief, item by item: held, changed in the room, missed, overtaken, or not on the card. The next card's cuts come from that record.
