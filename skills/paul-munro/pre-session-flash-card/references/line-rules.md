# Line rules

These rules decide what reaches the card and what leaves it.

## Name where the session time came from

Fix the session date and time before anything else, and name the source on the card: the calendar invite, your own words with their date, or inferred from the pattern. A card built on an inferred date carries the word "preliminary" in its header. One card was built for the wrong day because two records of the session time disagreed and neither was named on the card.

## The test for every line

Ask: does this need the client's judgment in real time, or does it just need the client's action? Judgment goes in the room. Action goes in the follow-up email.

- A decision that changes what happens next earns a line.
- A reminder that something is outstanding earns no line, however overdue it is.
- Outstanding admin never enters the session, so it never goes on the card. If an item is short of mission critical, it goes in the email after the session. The session is for coaching and deep work.

## Confirm an owed item is still owed

Before any owed item reaches a card, confirm the client still owes it. Email is an incomplete record of what a client has received. Check delivery, and where the visible channel is silent, ask the client. A ledger that logs commitments without logging when they were resolved manufactures fake debt.

## Protect the block by its position

Write the run sheet from the protected block outward. Place it first or second, then fit the deadline items around it. On three cards in a row the block sat after the deadline items with a note asking to protect it, and it ran last and short all three times. The slot is what protects it.

## Give every line that might resolve its skip condition

Any line that might resolve before the session carries its skip condition, written in the same line: "If it is in the folder by Thursday, skip." One line was overtaken two hours after the call, and the card had no instruction for it.

## Plan for the event that did not happen

Any block built on an event that may not have happened carries one line for the case that it did not: "If it did not happen: date it, move on." One block was built on a conversation that had not yet taken place.

## Two strikes and an item leaves the card

An item that has sat on two consecutive cards without being raised moves to the recap email. It returns to a card only if the client raises it. One item sat on two consecutive cards without being raised, and reached the client in three sentences of email instead. The card was carrying it as a wish.

## The run sheet caps your items and leaves room for theirs

The clock times cap your own items. The client's items get the open space. Write one labelled slot for the client's items of at least 20 minutes, placed where the transcripts show their material tends to arrive, for example just after the first five minutes. One session ran about two thirds on material the client brought, and the card had no room for it. That is the good outcome, and the card should be shaped for it. The slot lives inside the run sheet as one of its lines.

## Decisions stand on their own lines

Anything that must leave the session decided gets its own numbered line in "What the client decides today." A rider attached to another decision becomes its own line or comes off the card. Nothing mandatory sits below that block.

## Nothing invented is settled

Nothing invented enters the card as settled. Every proposed item is tagged PROPOSED and decided by the coach before the session. Once ruled, the line states the ruling and its date.

## Length and cuts

One page preferred, two pages maximum, about 1,100 words as the ceiling. One card ran about 1,900 words, and its run sheet did not survive the first ten minutes. When you cut, cut whole items. Keep the sentence that says what each remaining thing is.

## The retired list

A "collect before you hang up" list was tried and retired. It repeated the card's decisions as fragments with no context, and it slipped follow-up requests into the room. Leave it off.
