# Listen-fors

Three per card, chosen by where the engagement is. These are patterns in how the client talks, not topics to cover.

## Early engagement (sessions 1 to 4)

- Which problem they describe first when asked an open question. It is rarely the one they hired you for.
- Whether they describe their business in client names or in categories. Names mean no repeatable offer yet.
- How they answer "who decides". If it is always them, delegation is the constraint, whatever the stated goal is.
- Numbers they cannot produce without checking. A founder who does not know their close rate from memory is not running on it.

## Mid engagement (the long middle)

- The thing deferred to next session twice. The second deferral is the finding.
- A number they used to quote and have stopped quoting. People stop citing metrics that have turned.
- New vocabulary that arrived from somewhere else: a podcast, a peer group, a competitor. Usually a sign they are shopping for a different answer than the one you are working on.
- Who they have started blaming. A client, a contractor, a partner, the market. Blame moves outward when progress stalls.
- Energy mismatch: flat delivery of something that should be good news.

## Late engagement, or renewal in view

- Whether they describe the changes as theirs or as yours. "We decided" is the goal. "You told me to" means it will not survive the engagement ending.
- Whether they are bringing new problems or relitigating old ones.
- Mentions of time, cost or scope that are not about the work.
- Whether the next prediction extends past the end of the engagement.

## Stalled or circling

- The same three listen-fors appearing three sessions running.
- Questions asked that were answered in a previous session.
- Agreement that comes too fast.
- Rescheduling. Two moved sessions in a row is data, not logistics.

## How to pick

Take one from the engagement's current stage, one from whatever the last prediction revealed, and one from the thing you have been avoiding raising. The third is the one that earns the session.

Write each as a pattern you could notice in the moment, not a question to ask. "Does he name the partner when describing the bottleneck" works. "Discuss the partnership" does not.
