Applies before every session with a client you coach or advise on a recurring basis. Produces one page, the flash card, that you read while you talk and run the hour from.

## The play

1. Fix the session date and time, and name the source on the card: the calendar invite, your own words with their date, or inferred from the pattern. An inferred date puts the word "preliminary" in the header.
2. Read the transcript of the last session, the recap thread you sent, and the last debrief's notes on how the previous card held up.
3. Sort every candidate line with one test: does it need the client's judgment in real time, or only their action? Judgment goes in the room. Action goes in the follow-up email. Confirm any owed item is still owed before it reaches the card. Full rules: `references/line-rules.md`.
4. Write the sections in the fixed order in `references/card-sections.md`. Anything you invented goes in "Decide these before you dial", tagged PROPOSED, in plain words: what it is, your recommendation, adopt or kill. The coach rules before the session.
5. Build the run sheet from the protected block outward. Place the development block first or second, then fit the deadline items around it. The clock times cap your own items. Give the client one labelled slot of at least 20 minutes, placed where the transcripts show their material tends to arrive.
6. Branch on what you know going in:
   - A line might resolve before the session: write its skip condition in the same line. "If it is in the folder by Thursday, skip."
   - A block rests on an event that may not have happened: add one line for that case. "If it did not happen: date it, move on."
   - An item has sat on two consecutive cards without being raised: move it to the recap email. It returns only if the client raises it.
   - The client sent their own agenda note, and you have agreed the hour is theirs to set: use the client-led order in `references/card-sections.md`.
7. Check every line against `references/plain-language.md`. Then check length: one page preferred, two maximum, about 1,100 words as the ceiling. Cut whole items. Keep the sentence that says what each remaining thing is.
8. If a pre-read would help, build it after the card. The card still runs the hour alone.
9. After the session, record in your debrief how the card held up: held, changed in the room, missed, overtaken, or not on the card. The next card's cuts come from there.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: whether each owed item is still owed. Email is an incomplete record of what a client has received, so I check delivery, and ask where that channel is silent. A ledger that logs commitments without logging resolution manufactures fake debt.
- The mediocre version is long and protects its best block with a note. One card ran about 1,900 words and its run sheet did not survive the first ten minutes. On three cards in a row the development block sat after the deadline items with a note to protect it, and ran last and short all three times. The slot is what protects it.
- I know the card is right when I can run the hour from it with nothing else open, and every line needs the client's judgment in the room.
- I know the session went well when the client's material fills the open slot. One ran about two thirds on what the client brought. That is the good outcome, and the card should be shaped for it.
- Red flag: an item riding from card to card. One sat on two consecutive cards without being raised, and reached the client in three sentences of email instead. I was carrying it as a wish.

## Rules

- MUST build the card from the transcript of the last session and the sent recap thread.
- MUST give each decision that has to leave the session its own numbered line. Nothing mandatory sits below the decisions block.
- MUST tag every invented item PROPOSED until the coach rules. Then the line states the ruling and its date.
- MUST keep the numbers. Plain language simplifies the words around a number.
- NEVER build the card from a debrief alone.
- NEVER put outstanding admin on the card.
- NEVER place the development block last in the run sheet.
- NEVER add sections, including a "collect before you hang up" list. That list was retired because it repeated the card's decisions as fragments with no context and slipped follow-up requests into the room.
- NEVER show the card to the client.
