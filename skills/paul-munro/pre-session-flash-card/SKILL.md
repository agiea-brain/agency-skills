Applies minutes before a recurring session with a client you already know. Produces a one-page card you can read in two minutes and run the session from.

## The play

1. Open the client folder and read only three things: the last session write-up, anything they sent since, and the standing goal for the engagement. Nothing older unless the last write-up points you there.
2. Write the opener. One question, specific to the most consequential thing from last session, that cannot be answered with "good". "Did the pricing conversation with the retainer client happen?" beats "how was your week". The card carries one opener, not a list.
3. Surface the last prediction verbatim, with its date, and what actually happened. Every session ends with the client predicting a concrete outcome by a date, which is what makes this card possible at all. The mechanics of capturing and scoring them are in references/prediction-loop.md.
4. Branch on how the prediction resolved:
   - Hit: do not dwell. One sentence of credit, then push the next commitment bigger. A client who hits every prediction is predicting too small.
   - Missed, and they know: the card's job is to stop you relitigating. Note the miss, make the opener about the obstacle, not the outcome.
   - Missed, and they have not mentioned it: this is the session. Flag it on the card so you raise it in the first five minutes rather than at minute fifty.
   - Unresolved or no date attached: the real finding is a prediction that was never specific enough to resolve. Fix the format at the end of this session.
   - No prediction on file (first few sessions, or you missed one): run the card without it and make this session's close the first one.
5. Pick three things to listen for, no more. These are patterns, not topics: the thing they keep deferring, the number they stopped quoting, the person they have started blaming. Pull from references/listen-fors.md by where the engagement is.
6. Lay it out as one page in the fixed order in references/card-template.md. The order matters more than the content. Same shape every week means you stop reading it and start using it.
7. Close the session by capturing the next prediction straight into the folder. A card with no prediction next week means this step got skipped.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: what they did not say since the last session. A client who sent nothing in two weeks on a thing they were fired up about has gone quiet for a reason, and that is the opener.
- The mediocre version: a summary of last session. Accurate, comprehensive, useless. It tells you what you already discussed instead of what to do in the next hour.
- I know the card is right when I can read it in two minutes and have an opening question I am slightly nervous to ask.
- I know the session landed when the next prediction is harder than the last one and they set the date themselves.
- Red flag: three sessions in a row where the listen-fors are the same three items. The engagement is circling, and that is a conversation about the engagement, not another session.

## Rules

- MUST fit on one page. Two pages means it will not be read before the call.
- MUST quote the last prediction in the client's own words, with its date.
- MUST carry exactly one opener and exactly three listen-fors.
- MUST raise an unmentioned missed prediction in the first five minutes.
- MUST end every session with a new prediction written into the folder before the call drops.
- NEVER build the card from memory of the client. Build it from the folder, every time.
- NEVER let the card become the agenda. It is what you walk in with, not what you walk through.
