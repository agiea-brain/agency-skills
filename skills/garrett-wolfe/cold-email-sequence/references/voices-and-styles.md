# More Voices, Episodes and 2025-26 Styles

Practitioners and styles beyond the core sources in `playbook.md` §1. Researched 2026-10-26. Many of
these people publish their real frameworks behind paywalls (Jed Mahrle, Florin Tatulea, Caliber
courses) or only in audio, so the public material is thinner than their reputations. Numbers are
self-reported unless noted.

---

## 1. Voices worth stealing from

### Jordan Crawford (Blueprint GTM): PQS and PVP
The strongest new idea in the space. "Target pain, not persona."
- **PQS, Pain-Qualified Segment:** find the situation that made past customers buy, then a **public,
  detectable signal** that a prospect is in that situation now (hiring, tool changes, DNS records,
  filings, competitor footprint, support-doc changes). Build the list from the signal, not from
  firmographics. The email names the situation back to them. "If the pain doesn't produce a
  detectable signal, it isn't a PQS, it's a hypothesis."
- **PVP, Permissionless Value Proposition:** "a message that's worth something to the person who
  gets it", with no demo, signup or even a reply needed. Built by combining 2-5 data sources
  only you have.
  - Work backwards: who receives it → what data only we have → what work we can do for them.
  - If the number is too small to matter, send it further up the chain (to the PE firm, not a single
    portco).
  - **Customer test:** show it to a real customer. It passes only if they say "that would be really
    valuable to me."
  - **Gate test:** for each value sentence, could I say this without reading their file? If yes, it
    fails.
- His example: "We flew a drone over your San Jose location last week. We found unattended propane
  tanks in the back. That's an OSHA violation. Photo attached." To a PE firm: "Your 12 portcos' top 3
  ingredients are cinnamon, cane sugar and oat flour."
- Sources: edge.blueprintgtm.com; SaaS Unbound podcast "Ship an A+ campaign in 1 hour" (Sep 2025).

### Armand Farrokh & Nick Cegelski (30 Minutes to President's Club, *Cold Calling Sucks*)
The **4-sentence email**: reason you're reaching out → the problem → the solution → a low-friction
interest CTA. Plain text, a trigger-based subject, a short same-thread bump, then vary each later
follow-up. Template from their toolkit (Anthony Natoli, Lattice):

> Hey {{first_name}}, people leaders like you are moving to a more continuous performance and
> feedback culture. Because of this, employee growth and retention are top of mind. We help our
> customers build flexible performance management programs, which enables ongoing feedback from
> managers and employees. Is this on your radar at all?

E3 is a reply in the thread: problem → why it hurts → customer quote → "Open to learning more?"

### Sam McKenna (#samsales, "Show Me You Know Me")
- A subject only the recipient would understand: details joined with "+" ("switzerland + liip
  cheeseburger + [company]").
- Lead with a specific problem and **pre-empt the most likely objection in the body**.
- No calendar links: "do you have time over the next week or two to learn more?"
- About 20 emails a week. Her 20% reply rate comes from a low-volume, heavy-research motion and
  doesn't transfer to scaled sends. Use the subject and objection moves for tier-1 accounts.

### Kyle Coleman (Clari, Copy.ai, ClickUp): POV-led outbound
"Personalization that only shows research, without showing understanding, doesn't work." The email
**interprets** the signal ("here's what that hire means for you") instead of citing it.
- Construction: trigger → interpretation → copy.
- The 5-touch cascade from one strong POV:
  1. the strongest insight;
  2. a second painful implication;
  3. social proof plus a short story;
  4. a low-pressure invite;
  5. a graceful exit.
- Research with 5×5×5: 5 minutes, 5 insights, 5 minutes writing.
- Rank signals: strong signals create operational or strategic pressure; hires and awards are only
  background.

### Nick Abraham (Leadbird, 1.5M+ emails a month)
- "The number one thing that correlates to the result of your campaign is just the offer."
- Under 100 words; copy "gets fatigued way quicker" above 120.
- Strip legal suffixes ("LLC") from company names, since they signal automation.
- Personalize **one variable**, not the whole email: "I wasn't sure if I should reach out to you or
  Liam."
- "Is that something you'd be interested in?" is his best CTA, and now overused, so vary it.
- **Lead magnets and free mockups raise replies but convert to meetings less reliably.** That's
  the counterweight to PVP: grade on meetings.
- His bars: 2-4% reply, 20-40% of replies positive, one qualified lead per 350-500 contacts. Under
  1% reply usually means a deliverability problem, not a copy problem.

### Jack Reamer & Jeremy Chatelaine ("Cold Email Outreach with Jeremy & Jack", weekly teardowns)
- Say **why** you're writing in line 1, with an explicit "because".
- Frame the offer as a gift or sample rather than an ask.
- Back pay-on-results claims with specific proof.
- Test a stripped version with only the pain and the CTA.
- Test asking for feedback instead of pitching.
- "Gun to your head" offer exercise: write the offer the prospect can't refuse.
- "Quick question, [name]" plus flattery reads as fake.

### Others in brief
- **Jed Mahrle** (Practical Prospecting): subject → line 1 context → line 2 value prop → line 3 CTA →
  optional P.S. "for 90% of cold emails".
- **Steli Efti** (Close): answer who you are, what you want and why them, in that order; one ask per
  email.
- **Michael Seibel** (YC): 3 sentences, Situation → Reason to care → Ask. A good founder-to-founder
  register.
- **Bryan Kreuzberger** (*Breakthrough Email*): the "appropriate person" email to 3-4 people at one
  company, naming the others you've emailed. Still a valid multi-thread routing move.
- **Florin Tatulea / Chris Orlob:** the "4-sentence cold email" (paywalled). Expect it to look like the
  30MPC template above.

## 3. 2025-26 styles: what they are and whether to use them

| Style | What it is | Evidence | Our call |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permissionless value (PVP) / show, don't tell** | Email 1 *contains* the deliverable: the finding, list, photo or audit result | Strong anecdotes (Crawford; Eric 1 positive per 70); Abraham: more replies, not always more meetings | **Use it when we hold data they can't easily get.** Grade on meetings. Framework in `frameworks.md` |
| **Signal-based / PQS** | The list is built from an observed trigger; line 1 names it | No controlled study; summit consensus "timing beats copy" | **Default for every list we can build this way** |
| **POV-led** | Interpret the signal: what it means for them | Clari before-and-after claim only | **Use it for exec and tier-1 accounts**; it's the insight line in Kellen's 4-move |
| **Relevance over flattery** | No "loved your podcast"; a sharp problem statement or one variable | Gong: hours of personalization return little; Belkins personalized subjects 7% vs 3% | **House rule.** Read "personalization" in studies as relevance |
| **Ultra-short / text-like** | 3-4 sentences, under 80 words | Moderately strong and consistent across Smartlead, Gong and Instantly | **Default 50-90 words**; our niche winners run ~100 |
| **Lowercase subjects** | "new role", "field visits" | Open-rate data only (MPP-inflated); no reply evidence | **A voice choice, not a lever** |
| **5th-grade reading level** | Short words, short sentences | Traces to Boomerang 2016 (warm email); not replicated cold | **Grade ≤7 is a style norm**, not a promise of lift |
| **Loom / personal video** | Video in E1 or E2 | Vendor claims; adds a link; ~1 hour a day per rep | **Tier-1 only, and from E2** (no links in E1) |
| **AI microsites / custom pages** | A generated page per prospect | No reply data; vendors say it works best in email 2 | **Test from E2, never E1** |
| **"Assume it's AI" plain text** | No em dashes, no over-structure, text-only, no links in E1 | 69% of buyers dislike AI-sounding email (Hunter 2025) | **House rule** (lint plus humanizer) |

**Patterns that recur across all the new sources:**
1. Lead with a signal or a pain the reader recognizes.
2. One problem sentence, one proof point, and a yes/no interest CTA.
3. Under ~80-100 words, no pitch.
4. Where you hold unique data, give the finding, not an offer to make one.
5. Social proof in the body, never the subject.
