# Offers and Lead Magnets

What the prospect gets for replying. Adapted from Eric Nowoslawski's `lead-magnet-brainstorm`
(MIT) plus Spychalski and Braun.

"A concrete free offer outperforms 'book a call' by 3-10x" (Eric). A meeting is what *you* want;
the offer is what *they* get.

## Intake (answer before brainstorming)

1. What does the client sell, in one sentence a buyer would recognise?
2. What was the **#1 problem the best customer had before buying**? This is the north star, and its
   lever (make money, save time or money, reduce risk) is E1's lever.
3. What could we do for a prospect **in under 30 minutes** that they'd pay $100 for? Push back on
   "nothing". An SEO shop audits a page; a copywriter rewrites a subject line; a dev shop reviews
   one function; a data shop pulls 20 real leads.
4. Any legal, regulatory or client limits on what we can promise?
5. What would make them **not** reply? (Too busy, burned before, already have a vendor, sounds
   like a pitch.) The offer should answer the biggest one (Eric).

## The 10 archetypes

| | Archetype | Example | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Free audit / diagnostic | "a 5-minute audit of your [thing]" | "want me to run it?" |
| B | Data / research | "we looked at 500 [type] firms; here's what the top 10% do" | "want the summary?" |
| C | Competitive intel | "I pulled who engaged with your 3 biggest competitors' last 10 posts" | "want the list?" |
| D | Template / checklist | "the 12-point checklist we run before every launch" | "want me to send it?" |
| E | Intro / connection | "I know 3 [role]s who solved exactly this" (never fake it) | "want the intro?" |
| F | Quick-win work | "I'll write 3 subject lines for your ICP" | "reply with your ICP and I'll send them" |
| G | Specific-to-them analysis | 2-3 sentences of real observation (high-ACV) | "happy to go deeper if useful" |
| H | Tool / free account | "I can spin you up an account" | "want access?" |
| I | Working session | "15 min where I walk your checkout and flag 3 friction points" | "worth 15 min?" (E3+, never E1) |
| J | Benchmark | "you rank #X for [term]; the median in your space is #Y" | "want the full comparison?" |

Plus two that lower the risk of the first step:
- **Free test / pilot:** the first small piece of work, free (GEX's own front-end offer is a free
  test campaign). Ask: "What can they accomplish in 14 days that would make them not want to stop?"
- **Pay on result:** charge only after the outcome ("we remove the negative reviews; you pay only
  after removal" produced 1 positive reply per 70 emails).

## Scoring

Rate each idea 1-5 on four criteria:

| Criterion | 5 means |
|---|---|
| Cheap to deliver at scale | automated, or 5 minutes of manual work |
| Genuinely valuable | they'd pay $100+ for it |
| Shows competence | delivering it proves we can do the paid work |
| Unique | competitors aren't all offering it |

**15/20 or more: propose it. Under 15: rework it or drop it.** Recommend the top 2-3 by lowest
delivery friction, fit with intake question 2, and novelty. If every competitor does free audits,
pick something else.

## Rules

- **Never gate it behind a form.** Send it inline or as a direct link once they reply.
- **The prospect's maximum effort is a one-line reply** or 1-2 data points ("reply with your ICP").
- **Don't promise what can't be delivered** at the volume the campaign sends.
- "Free consultation" isn't an offer. "15 min where I [specific action] for you" is.
- The offer must use the client's real capabilities. Write the "what we do NOT do" line before
  brainstorming.
- **If a campaign underperforms, change what they get**, not the wording (Eric).

## Output format

For each idea: **what it is / what it takes to deliver / example CTA / score (x/20)**.
