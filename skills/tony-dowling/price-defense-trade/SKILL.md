Applies when a prospect has pushed on price and the rep is about to go back in. One principle governs everything: more for more, never less for the same. Produces a negotiation guide the rep can read in the car park, and a clean takeaway version to bring into the room.

## The play

1. Identify the client workspace the deal belongs to and work only inside its data from here.
2. Diagnose the real cause. Look up the deal and prospect in HubSpot (stage, amount, close date, contacts, notes) and read the recent call notes and transcripts for the account. Decide which of three it is: (a) a genuine budget constraint, (b) competitive pressure from a vendor undercutting, (c) procurement doing its job and testing the rep's nerve. State the diagnosis in one line with the evidence found. Thin notes: say so, and diagnose from what exists. Deal not found: ask the rep to confirm the deal or company name; do not guess.
3. Check standard terms. Open the client workspace's own Menu for Sale in the client brain to confirm standard commercial terms, deliverables, and what is and is not tradeable. More than one candidate document: use the one most clearly titled as the Menu for Sale and name it.
4. Write Part 1, the Value Re-Anchor Script: how to hold the line by tying the price to the cost of the prospect's unsolved problem or the risk of inaction, using this deal's own numbers and the prospect's own words from the notes.
5. Write Part 2, the Concession Trading Matrix: three trades to offer only if they insist, each "give X to get Y". Pull real deliverables and terms from the Menu for Sale so the trades are real. Patterns and worked trades in references/trading-matrix.md.
6. Write Part 3, the Walk-Away / Class Line: one professional boundary line that holds status without sounding desperate.
7. Deliver in chat, diagnosis first, three parts labelled, scripts in quotes. Then produce a self-contained takeaway document (PDF or doc) with the diagnosis, the three parts, and the matrix as a quick-reference table, and offer it alongside. Menu not located: fall back to generic but still commercial trades and flag that terms must be verified against the client's menu before the meeting.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: who raised the price. A buyer who has used the service before and a procurement officer who has never seen it are asking different questions, and the notes usually show which.
- Second tell: whether the rep has already offered something for nothing. If a discount is on the table with no trade, the first job is to attach a trade to it retrospectively.
- The mediocre version: "hold firm, remind them of the value", three trades that are all variations on a discount, and a walk-away line that sounds like a threat.
- I know the guide is right when every trade in the matrix is a term the firm actually offers, the re-anchor uses a number the prospect said themselves, and the rep could read the walk-away line to the prospect's chief executive without wincing.
- I know it worked when the deal closes at list or with a trade on the record, not with a discount and a hope.

## Rules

- MUST diagnose the cause before writing a script.
- MUST tie the re-anchor to this deal's own numbers and words.
- MUST make every trade "give X to get Y", with X and Y both real.
- MUST verify tradeable terms against the client's Menu for Sale, or flag that they are unverified.
- NEVER advise dropping price without taking something off the table or gaining a concession.
- NEVER guess at a deal that cannot be found. Ask.
- NEVER let the walk-away line read as a threat or an apology.
