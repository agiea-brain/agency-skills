# The Comeback Book framework

The Comeback Book is the team's own record of objections heard in market, each with three or four comeback versions that were workshopped and tested on real calls. It is the only source for scripts. This page holds the framework the book is built on, the mapping rules, and the capture process for new entries. It holds no scripts.

## Core brush-offs

Most books cover these five. Map the rep's wording to the closest one before searching for variants.

| Rep hears | Recorded objection |
|---|---|
| "Send me some info", "email me something", "put it in writing" | Send me info / an email |
| "We already use someone", "we're happy with our supplier", "we have that covered" | Already have someone / happy with incumbent |
| "Call me in the new year", "not now", "bad timing", "we're mid-project" | Call me back in 3 / 6 months / bad timing |
| "There's no money for that", "budgets are frozen", "not this year" | No budget right now |
| "I'd need to speak to my partner", "that's a board decision", "I'll raise it with the director" | Need to run it past my board / director / partner |

A variant maps to the nearest recorded objection. A genuinely new objection is one that does not fit any recorded entry, and it triggers capture rather than improvisation.

## The 4-part structure

Every recorded comeback is presented in this shape.

1. **Acknowledge and Validate.** Agree the brush-off is reasonable. Never defend, never argue. The prospect's guard drops when nothing pushes back.
2. **Pattern Interrupt / Re-frame.** A counter-intuitive commercial reality or hidden cost of inaction, in one sentence. It makes the automatic "no" feel slightly less safe than it did a second ago.
3. **Low-Friction Pivot.** Strictly to a 15-minute diagnostic Call 1, framed as low commitment: no pitch, no proposal, a short diagnosis they keep whatever happens.
4. **Word-for-word script.** The spoken version in natural rhythm, and a 3-line email version for when the brush-off arrived by email or the call ended first.

## Cheat-sheet layout

```
[Objection] · found in Comeback Book · [prospect, stage, context used, or "no prospect context"]

Say this
"[best recorded version, lightly adapted]"
Variant: "[recorded variant 2]"
Variant: "[recorded variant 3]"

Or email this
[line 1: acknowledge]
[line 2: re-frame]
[line 3: 15-minute ask with two time options]
```

## When the objection is not in the book

Say it plainly:

"This one is not in the Comeback Book yet, so there is no tested line to give you. Draft what you would say in your own words and it can be added for the team."

Then prompt the rep through the four parts as questions, not answers:

1. How would you agree with them without defending?
2. What is the cost of them doing nothing that they have not thought about?
3. How would you ask for 15 minutes so it feels like no commitment?
4. Say the whole thing out loud. Now shorten it to three lines for email.

Record the draft as a new entry marked "untested" so the next workshop can refine it. Do not present an untested draft as the team's comeback.

## Enrichment

Only from sources connected in this client workspace, and only to personalise, never to add arguments:

- CRM: deal stage, company size and industry, logged objections, recent notes.
- Call notes or transcripts: the most recent call's content, tone, and the pain the prospect surfaced.

Nothing found: proceed without it and say "no prospect context" in the header line.
