Applies in the seconds after a prospect brushes a rep off. An objection is an invitation to diagnose, not an argument to win; the goal is to disrupt the automatic brush-off and secure a 15-minute diagnostic Call 1, never to pitch or close. Produces a cheat-sheet built only from the team's own recorded language.

## The play

1. Parse the rep's message for the objection and any named prospect or company.
2. Open the document named "Comeback Book" in the client brain. It is the team's crowdsourced record of real objections heard in market, each with three or four workshop-tested comeback versions. Find the matching objection, or the closest recorded one if the rep's wording is a variant. The core brush-offs the book usually covers, and the mapping rules, are in references/comeback-framework.md.
   - Match found: present the best-fitting recorded version largely verbatim, lightly adapted to the prospect, plus two of the recorded variants to pick from.
   - No match: do not invent anything. Tell the rep plainly that this objection is not solved yet, and offer to capture a new entry by asking them to draft the response in their own words, with the 4-part structure as scaffolding only.
3. If a prospect is named, enrich from connected sources: the CRM for deal stage, notes, size, industry and logged objections; call notes for the most recent call's content, tone and surfaced pain. Nothing found or source not connected: proceed without it, silently.
4. Lay every recorded comeback on the 4-part framework the book is built around: Acknowledge and Validate; Pattern Interrupt or Re-frame; Low-Friction Pivot to a 15-minute Call 1; the word-for-word spoken script plus a 3-line email variant.
5. Personalise with the prospect's name and situation where context was found. Conversational, not corporate.
6. Deliver as a cheat-sheet: one line naming the objection, confirming it was found in the book, and the prospect context used; "Say this" with the script and variants; "Or email this" with the 3-line version. No preamble, no theory.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: whether the comeback pivots to Call 1 or to the pitch. A comeback that ends in "let me tell you what we do" has lost the call even if the prospect stays on the line.
- Second tell: whether the words are the team's or mine. Generic objection-handling language is instantly recognisable to a prospect who hears it four times a week, and it is not what this skill exists to produce.
- The mediocre version: a textbook "feel, felt, found" response, a paragraph of value proposition, and a close.
- I know the output is right when the rep could read "Say this" aloud without editing and it sounds like someone on their team, and the email version fits on a phone screen.
- I know the book is working when new entries arrive from the floor faster than the coach adds them.

## Rules

- MUST take every comeback from the Comeback Book.
- MUST pivot every comeback to a 15-minute diagnostic Call 1.
- MUST tell the rep plainly when an objection is not in the book, and offer to capture a new entry.
- NEVER invent a comeback line or fall back to generic objection handling.
- NEVER pitch or close inside a comeback.
- NEVER argue with the objection. Acknowledge first.
