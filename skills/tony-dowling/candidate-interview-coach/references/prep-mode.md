# /prep: the interview pack

## Red flag triggers

| Flag | Trigger in the CV or profile | Quote it as |
|---|---|---|
| Sales puffery | "Consistently exceeded", "rainmaker", "trusted advisor", superlatives without a number | The exact phrase, with the role it sits under |
| Job-hopping | Any sales role under 18 months, or three roles in four years | The role, dates, and calculated tenure |
| Ungrounded quota claim | A percentage with no quota value, no period, no team context | The claim as written |
| Round-number claim | "£1m", "200%", "doubled revenue" with nothing behind it | The claim as written |
| Inbound dressed as hunting | "Managed a pipeline of", "handled enquiries", "account growth" in a role described as new business | The phrase, and why it reads as inbound |
| Missing CRM | No CRM named across a career in sales | The absence, by role |

Each flag is listed with its quoted line. Flags are questions to ask, not reasons to reject on their own.

## Forensic commercial-maths questions

Four questions, each built to reconstruct a claim in the CV. The expected answer is a number; a story in place of a number is the finding.

| Dimension | Question shape | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation currency | "In your [role], how many times a week did you put pricing in front of a decision maker?" | A weekly number, and whether it changes when pressed |
| Conversion per stage | "Of those, how many became proposals, and how many of those closed?" | Ratios that multiply through to the claimed result |
| Average order value | "What was a typical deal worth, and what was the biggest? How many of the big ones a year?" | Whether the quota claim is a few large deals or a steady book |
| Origination | "Of the deals you closed in your best year, how many did you find yourself, cold, with no marketing lead?" | A fraction, and hesitation |

Then the cross-check, out loud: presentations × conversion × order value against the claimed quota attainment. If it does not multiply through, ask which number is wrong.

## 45-second cold-call roleplay

Pick one real offer from the Menu for Sale. Give the candidate: the offer in two lines, the persona (title and company type), and the single objective, which is to book a 15-minute diagnostic call, not to pitch.

Setup line: "You are calling [persona] at [company type]. You sell [offer, two lines]. You have 45 seconds. Your only objective is 15 minutes in the diary. Go."

The manager plays the prospect, gives one of the core brush-offs from the Comeback Book at the 20-second mark, and does not help.

## Post-run critique script

Delivered immediately after the first run, flat and specific, in these words:

"Thanks. Three things. You pitched before you asked a question. You asked for a meeting without saying how long or what they get from it. And when I said [brush-off], you argued with it. Take a minute and go again, same persona, same objective."

Listen on the second run for the only thing being tested: did they change the three things, or defend them? A candidate who says "I was getting to that" has told you how coaching will go.

## Accountability probes

1. "In this role you would hold 50 accounts, capped. An untouched account leaves your list after 30 days and goes to the house pool. How does that sit with you?"
2. "Every Friday at 15:30 you would sit with your manager and reconcile what you committed to on Monday against what happened. What would you want that meeting to be like?"
3. "If the CRM says you made two presentations this week and you say four, which is true?"
4. "Describe a time a manager held you to a number you had agreed and you missed it. What happened next?"

Listen for: acceptance with a question (good), enthusiasm with no question (check again later), negotiation of the terms before the job is offered (the finding).
