Applies when you have a founder or company in mind who is visibly good at something your audience wants to learn. Produces one post that teaches the tactic, tags them, and asks your readers to follow them.

A shoutout does three jobs, in this order: it starts a relationship with the person tagged, it shows your audience you can tell good work from bad, and it puts the post in front of the tagged person's audience. It does not book calls. That is fine; it makes the posts that do book calls land harder.

## The play

1. Pick the target. All five must be true; the full screen is in references/target-and-research.md.
   - B2B founder or company your audience overlaps with.
   - 10K to 100K followers. Under 10K the tag reaches too few people. Far over 100K your post is noise to them.
   - Visibly good at one thing in your field, not "great overall".
   - Verifiable: you can point to posts, pages or public numbers.
   - Not someone you have critiqued in public. Never both.
2. Research before writing. Read their last 15 to 20 posts, their site, their newsletter if they have one, and any interviews. Find 3 to 5 specific things they do well, each with a piece of evidence. Use the checklist in references/target-and-research.md.
3. Write the post in this shape:
   - Hook: "[Name] is doing [specific thing] better than almost anyone I've seen."
   - Who they are: 2 to 3 lines, max.
   - The breakdown, 60 to 70% of the post: "Here's what they do right:" then 3 to 5 numbered patterns. Each one is what they do, the evidence, and why it works.
   - Why it matters: 1 to 2 lines turning it into a principle the reader can apply.
   - CTA: "Give [Name] a follow. This is what good looks like."
4. Write the first comment. It goes one level deeper on a single pattern from the post. No link to anything you sell.
5. Run the check in references/post-check.md. Any fail, rewrite before posting.
6. Branch by what they are good at:
   - Content: name cadence, format mix, hook pattern, and their best post with its numbers.
   - Outbound or email: only what is visible from outside (the messages you received, their public talks). Never guess at their numbers.
   - Funnel or site: walk the path a buyer takes and name the step that is unusually good.
7. Post at most one shoutout a week. Weeks between shoutouts are what make them read as real.

Worked examples are in references/worked-examples.md. Open them before writing your first one.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: whether the tagged person would screenshot it and share it. If they would not, it is too vague or it has a hidden "but".
- The mediocre version: "Huge respect for [Name], their content is fire." Praise with no tactic. Nobody learns anything, nobody shares it, and the tagged person gives it a like at most.
- The other mediocre version: a teardown with a compliment on top. "They do X well, but they could also..." That is a critique, and the subject feels used.
- I know the post is right when a reader who has never heard of the subject could copy one of the patterns tomorrow, and every claim has a link behind it.
- I know it landed when the subject comments or reshares within 48 hours, and their followers start connecting with you.

## Rules

- MUST back every claim with a post, page or public number you can link.
- MUST make the only CTA "follow them".
- MUST keep the breakdown to 3 to 5 patterns, each with evidence.
- NEVER mention your own offer, services, clients or a call link, in the post or the first comment.
- NEVER add "but", "gap", "opportunity" or "what I'd add". That is a different post.
- NEVER claim numbers about them that they have not published.
- NEVER shout out someone you have publicly critiqued, and never the same person twice.
- NEVER post more than one shoutout a week.
