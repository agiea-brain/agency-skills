Applies to any copy a busy buyer will read on a phone between meetings. Produces a rewrite at a 5th-grade reading level and a short check sheet showing it passes.

Simple copy is not dumb copy. A blunt point survives at grade 5. Pretension does not.

## The play

1. Read the draft out loud. Mark every place you stumble or take a breath mid-sentence.
2. Cut sentence length. Average 14 words or fewer. No sentence over 22. Split on "and", "which", "because" and commas.
3. One idea per sentence. If a sentence holds a "which" or a "that" clause plus a second point, it is two sentences.
4. Swap long words. Two syllables or fewer wherever the meaning holds. Run the swap list in references/word-swaps.md.
5. Voice and rhythm. Active voice only. Contractions on. No semicolons. Few dashes. A period beats a comma, and a comma beats anything else.
6. Keep the exceptions. Do not simplify:
   - Proper nouns: names, companies, products, job titles.
   - Terms the reader uses every day: ARR, CRM, ICP, SDR, pipeline. Match the reader. A CFO reads "EBITDA". A shop owner does not.
   - Direct quotes from the prospect, a case study or a source.
7. Run the check in references/check-sheet.md and attach the pass/fail lines to the rewrite.
8. Branch by format:
   - Cold email and DMs: be strictest here. Under 90 words for a first touch. The reader owes you nothing.
   - Posts: the hook line must pass on its own. Many people read only that.
   - Landing pages: headline and button text first. Body copy can run a little longer if each sentence still passes.
   - Call scripts: read them aloud at speaking pace. Spoken lines should be shorter than written ones.

Before and after pairs are in references/word-swaps.md. Open them when a rewrite starts to sound childish rather than clear.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: the longest sentence. It is almost always where the writer was hiding a point they were not sure about.
- The mediocre version: the copy is "proofread". Spelling is clean, but it still says "leverage our robust methodology to streamline your pipeline". Clean and unreadable.
- The other mediocre version: overcorrected into baby talk. Short words, but also no numbers, no names, no specifics. Grade 5 keeps the specifics.
- I know the copy is right when an 11-year-old could explain back what you offer, and the expert you are writing to does not feel talked down to.
- I know it worked when replies quote your words back to you. People only quote what they understood.

## Rules

- MUST keep average sentence length at 14 words or fewer, and no sentence over 22.
- MUST keep every number, name and specific claim from the original.
- MUST read it aloud before calling it done.
- NEVER simplify a proper noun, a quote, or a term the reader uses daily.
- NEVER use a word from the swap list when the plain word means the same thing.
- NEVER use semicolons in outreach copy.
