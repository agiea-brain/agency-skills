Applies to short-form copy that has to land in one read. Produces a punched rewrite, alternates, and a passing score sheet, or a clear statement of the missing fact blocking the pass.

Verbose copy is a confidence problem: every extra word is the writer hedging against being wrong. Decide what the one claim is, state it in the fewest words that still land, and prove it fast. First say it straight, then say it great. Never skip straight.

## The play

1. Check the length. If it's the body of a blog post or newsletter, say so: long-form copy should flow, and this would chop it. Offer to sharpen its headline or opening line instead.
2. If the client has a brand voice document, read it first. The rubric stays the same; the register follows the client.
3. Name the job. What should this piece make the reader do or feel, and in what medium? If the user didn't say, infer it and state the assumption. Pull the word budget from references/rubric.md, using the closest row.
4. Diagnose. Name the bloat patterns present and quote the offending words, in one or two lines. The patterns are in references/bloat-patterns.md.
5. Say it straight. State the one claim in plain English. Never invent a claim the source doesn't support. If the copy has no real claim, that is the finding: say so at the top, ask for the missing fact, and still give a best attempt marked as failing.
6. Say it great. Rewrite with one pattern from references/hook-toolbox.md, never three stacked. Cut 30%, then restore the one line you miss (on a single line, the one word you miss). Before the first rewrite, read references/calibration-pairs.md for the size of the cut.
7. Write one or two alternates on a different angle, each with a different toolbox pattern. A synonym swap doesn't count.
8. Score the primary in a separate pass from drafting, using references/rubric.md: hard gates first, then the six dimensions. Score pessimistically, take the lower number when torn, and quote the offending text for any dimension below full marks.
9. Revise until it passes. Fix gates first, then the lowest dimensions, then re-score everything, because cutting for economy often breaks the hook. Maximum three cycles.
10. Still failing after three cycles means the straight message is wrong or a real specific is missing. Say which, and ask for the number or the moment.
11. Deliver in this format:

```
DIAGNOSIS: 1-2 lines, the bloat patterns found, quoted
PRIMARY: the punched rewrite
ALTERNATES:
A) different angle - toolbox pattern used
B) different angle - toolbox pattern used
PUNCH SCORE: __/60 (Hook __, Economy __, Concreteness __, Claim __, Rhythm __, Single __) PASS or FAIL
GATES: clean, or the gate failed and the words that failed it
BIGGEST CHANGE: one line
BLOCKING (only on a fail): the one line or missing fact stopping the pass
```

## What good looks like

- First thing to check that most people skip: whether there is a claim at all. Most bloated copy is padding wrapped around nothing a reasonable person could disagree with, and no amount of cutting fixes that.
- The mediocre version: the same sentence with a third of the words removed and three hook tricks stacked on top. It's shorter and still says nothing, and the alternates are synonym swaps of the primary.
- The output is right when it reads aloud in one clean pass, a stranger would stop on line one, every number traces to the source, and it scores 48/60 or better with Hook Strength at 7 or higher.
- Labels and buttons can get longer and still be better. "Learn More" losing to "Get the pricing math" is a punch-up.

## Rules

- MUST say it straight before saying it great.
- MUST score in a separate pass from drafting, and run the hard gates before the dimensions.
- MUST pass at 48/60 with no dimension below 6 and Hook Strength at 7 or higher.
- MUST stop at three revision cycles and name what is blocking.
- NEVER fabricate a number, name, or claim, including claims about the reader. Leave a placeholder and surface the gap.
- NEVER present a failing rewrite as finished. Deliver the best draft, its score sheet, and the blocking line.
- NEVER carry a number from the calibration pairs into real copy.
- NEVER use this on long-form body copy.
