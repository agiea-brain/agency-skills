# Bloat patterns and directness principles

Open this for the diagnosis (step 4). Name the patterns present and quote the words.

## The nine bloat patterns

1. **Throat-clearing intros.** "In the world of agency growth, one challenge stands out..." Fix: delete the first sentence, then check whether the second goes too.
2. **Hedging qualifiers.** "This can often help you potentially reduce..." Fix: commit to the claim or cut it.
3. **Explaining the benefit instead of stating it.** "By systematizing delivery, you create efficiencies that free up founder time." Fix: state the outcome as a picture. "You stop reviewing every deliverable at 9pm."
4. **Abstract nouns.** "Operational excellence," "strategic clarity." Fix: swap each for the thing it looks like in real life (a calendar, a Friday, an invoice).
5. **Buried verbs.** "The implementation of a standardized process results in the reduction of churn." Fix: actor plus live verb in the first five words. "Standardize onboarding and churn drops."
6. **Saying it twice.** "Fast and efficient, saving you time and effort." Fix: keep the sharper twin.
7. **Over-transitioning.** "Additionally... Furthermore..." Fix: a paragraph break is a transition.
8. **Adjectives doing proof's job.** "Incredible results." Fix: replace the adjective with a number, a name, or a timeframe.
9. **The soft landing.** Ending on a summary. Fix: cut the last line and see if the piece got stronger.

## The directness principles

1. Lead with the sharpest claim. Readers grade you on line one.
2. One idea per sentence, one claim per piece.
3. Concrete beats abstract. If you can't photograph it, sharpen it. "Reclaim your time" becomes "Get your Fridays back."
4. Verb-forward. Kill implementation, optimization, utilization.
5. Specifics beat adjectives. "Dramatically shorten your sales cycle" becomes "Close in one call instead of five."
6. Claim, then proof, within one line.
7. Every line earns the next line.
8. Read it aloud. Out of breath means split it. Two short sentences that are really a setup and payoff get joined with "but," "and," or "so." The goal is punch, and fragments aren't punch.
9. Talk about the reader's business rather than your method. Count the "you"s against the "we"s.
