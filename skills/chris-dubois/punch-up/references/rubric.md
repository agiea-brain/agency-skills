# Punch-up rubric

Open this to set the word budget (step 3) and to score (step 8).

## Word budgets

Targets, not gates. Going over is a flag to cut.

| Unit | Budget |
|------|--------|
| Headline | under 10 words |
| Hero (headline + subhead) | under 25 words |
| Subhead | one sentence |
| Email subject line | 40-50 characters |
| Email opener | under 15 words |
| LinkedIn hook | under 12 words |
| CTA button | 3-6 words, verb-first |
| Tagline | under 8 words |

## Hard gates

Any failure is an automatic fail. Fix first, then score.

- **G1.** No em dashes.
- **G2.** No AI vocabulary: leverage, utilize, delve, unlock, elevate, empower, streamline, seamless, robust, holistic, synergy, game-changing, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, next-level, supercharge, navigate (as a metaphor), landscape (as a metaphor), "in today's fast-paced world," "at the end of the day."
- **G3.** No antithesis constructions: "It's not X. It's Y." or "That's not X. That's Y."
- **G4.** No fabricated specifics. Every number, name and claim traces to the source copy or the user, including claims about the reader. If one is missing, surface the gap and leave a placeholder.
- **G5.** Any CTA matches the brand publishing it. If you don't know the brand, mark this gate not checked.

## The six dimensions

Score each 0-10. The anchors mark 10, 7 and 3; score between them where a piece falls between them.

**PASS: 48/60, no dimension below 6, and Hook Strength at 7 or higher.** The hook floor is higher because a piece that scores 9 everywhere with a 6 hook never gets read.

1. **Hook Strength.** Does the first line force the second? 10: a stranger stops scrolling. 7: clear and relevant, but a competitor could have written it. 3: opens with setup or a category statement.
2. **Word Economy.** Could you cut 20% without losing meaning? 10: cutting anything breaks it. 7: two or three units carry padding. 3: a 30% cut would improve it.
3. **Concreteness.** Can the reader photograph it? 10: numbers, named objects, real scenes. 7: one or two specifics, but abstractions carry key claims. 3: category language throughout.
4. **Claim Sharpness.** Does it say one thing a reasonable person could disagree with? 10: committed and falsifiable, stated flat and backed fast. 7: a real claim, softened until nobody would argue. 3: safe observations.
5. **Rhythm and Read-Aloud.** Does it sound like a confident person talking? 10: varied lengths, reads aloud in one clean pass. 7: flat, or one clunky stretch. 3: run-ons or choppy fragments.
6. **Single-Mindedness.** One idea, one enemy, one action? 10: everything serves one claim and one next step. 7: a side point or second CTA leaks attention. 3: claims fighting for the lead.

## Repair moves

- Low Hook: rewrite line one from the piece's sharpest fragment, or apply a toolbox pattern.
- Low Economy: run the cut-30% pass.
- Low Concreteness: swap an abstraction for something you can photograph.
- Low Claim: find the one thing a reasonable person could argue with and state it flat.
- Low Rhythm: read it aloud, then join or split.
- Low Single-Mindedness: cut the second idea.
