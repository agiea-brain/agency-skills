# Worked example

Everything here is invented for illustration, including the agency, the buyer document and the scores. Use it to see the shape and the standard of a read, and never quote it as evidence.

**The piece:** the homepage for Northline, an agency selling websites and local ads to HVAC contractors.

**The hero:** "It's 2pm on a Tuesday in July and your phones are dead. Northline fills your schedule with AI-powered demand generation."

**The rest of the page:** a three-step "Our Process" section, a testimonial, and a "Book a strategy call" button. It shows no work samples and no price.

**Buyer document lines used:**

- Section 1: "Owns a nine-truck HVAC company, second generation, age 51. Names hiring techs as his hardest problem."
- Section 5: "Most find vendors through Google, on a phone, between calls."
- Section 2: "Busiest weeks are July and January. Slow months are April and October."
- Section 3: "Won't call a marketing company without seeing a contractor's site they built."
- Section 4: "Got burned on a $2,500/mo contract with no idea what he was paying for."
- Sales call, March: "Don't tell me about demand gen. Tell me how many calls."

## The read

**Persona.** Rick Sobol, 51, runs a nine-truck HVAC company his father started (ICP, section 1). He's on his phone in the van between calls, and he found Northline through a Google search for HVAC marketing (ICP, section 5). His last agency cost him $2,500 a month and he never learned what it did (ICP, section 4).

### Findings that cost conversions

**1. The opening scene tells Rick these people have never run an HVAC company.**
A July Tuesday is the week his phones don't stop. He'd be turning work away, and the page opens on the one afternoon that can't happen to him.
Evidence: "Busiest weeks are July and January. Slow months are April and October." (ICP, section 2)
Fix: move the scene to April. This one is the writer's mistake.

**2. He can't find a site Northline built, so he leaves.**
The page has no work samples, and that's the first thing he looks for.
Evidence: "Won't call a marketing company without seeing a contractor's site they built." (ICP, section 3)
Fix: show two contractor sites directly under the hero. If none exist, the client has to decide what to show.

**3. There's no price, and that repeats his worst vendor experience.**
Evidence: "Got burned on a $2,500/mo contract with no idea what he was paying for." (ICP, section 4)
Fix: state a starting price and what it includes.

### Worth fixing

**4. "Demand generation" is seller vocabulary in the hero.**
Evidence: "Don't tell me about demand gen. Tell me how many calls." (Sales call, March)
Fix: name calls booked.

**5. The page ignores what he names first.**
Evidence: "Names hiring techs as his hardest problem." (ICP, section 1)
Northline can't solve hiring, so this belongs in the sales conversation and stays off the page.

### What to cut

The "Our Process" section pays for the work samples and the price. Rick didn't ask how the agency works, and nothing in the source documents says he cares.

### Questions for the client

Can Northline publish a starting price, and which two sites can it show?

### Cut in the evidence pass

The page's green color felt off, but no source supports that, so it's gone.
