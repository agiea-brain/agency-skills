# Rewrite diff worksheet

Open this at step 3, and again every time the user edits a draft after the skill is live. A rewrite shows what the person refuses to send, which the sent mail alone cannot show.

## Finding the rewrites

- Ask the user for two or three AI drafts they edited, with the version they sent.
- Or search their drafts and sent mail for near-identical pairs written within a day of each other.
- Or skip this step and pick it up at step 9, where the test drafts become the first rewrites.

## Diffing one rewrite

Go line by line and sort every change into one of five columns:

| Change | What to record | Example of what it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | the exact words removed | an avoid-list entry |
| Added | the exact words added | a pattern, or a phrase to reach for |
| Softened | before and after | an ask or a deadline rule |
| Hardened | before and after | where the person is blunter than the AI |
| Moved | what moved and where | an order rule (commitments before asks, price early) |

Then answer three questions about the whole email:
1. What kind of recipient was it? The rule belongs to that register until the sent mail shows it holds elsewhere.
2. Did the person give a reason, in the edit or out loud? A stated reason outranks an inferred one.
3. Did they leave something rough on purpose: a typo, a run-on, a repeated phrase? Record it as kept, so the next draft does not smooth it away.

## Turning the diff into the skill

- A change seen once is a pattern for that register, marked with how many times it has been seen.
- A change seen in two different registers becomes a general rule.
- When a rewrite and the sent mail disagree, the sent mail wins on frequency. The rewrite still wins for the exact kind of email it came from.
- Every change goes into the patterns section. A learning-log line alone is not read at draft time, so it does not change the next draft.

## What diffs usually turn up

These come up often enough to check for on purpose:
- The AI draft was longer than anything the person sends to that recipient.
- The AI used a word the person never uses ("leverage", "seamless", "unlock").
- The AI wrote an ask as an instruction where the person writes it as an offer.
- The AI committed someone else's time ("my colleague and I will review it").
- The AI apologised at length where the person apologises in three words.
- The AI used a link from the signature or an old thread that was not the right one.
- The AI polished away a habit the person keeps on purpose.
