# Interview questions

Open this at step 6, after the counting. Ask three or four at a time. After each group, play back what you heard in a few lines and let the user correct it. People correct a paraphrase far more readily than they volunteer detail.

Skip any question the sent mail already answered. Ask it as a confirmation instead: "Your mail shows X. Is that right?"

## Who and how

1. Who do you write to most in a normal week? Name the kinds of people, not individuals.
2. Which of those need a different tone from you? What changes?
3. Is there anyone you write to more formally than your mail shows, or less?
4. What does a good reply from you do for the other person? Listening for: speed, clarity, options, warmth.

## What the mail cannot show

5. Which links do you send people to book time, and which one goes to whom? Paste them. These become the only links the skill may use.
6. Do you put prices in email, or only on calls? How do you round them?
7. Are there words or phrases you would never send? Anything an AI draft wrote that made you wince?
8. When someone cancels, apologises or goes quiet, what do you do?
9. When you say no to something, how do you say it?
10. What do you want people to be able to do with your email afterwards? Listening for: forward it internally, act without a call, reply in one line.

## About the drafts

11. Think of the last AI draft you rewrote. What was wrong with it?
12. What would make you send a draft without touching it?
13. Is there anything rough about how you write that you want kept? Typos, long sentences, asides.

## Close

14. What else should a person writing as you know that we have not covered?

Repeat the close until the answer is nothing.

## Where the answers go

| Answers | Land in |
|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | the register map |
| 5 | the verified-links list in the draft mechanics |
| 6, 8, 9 | patterns, each with its register |
| 7, 11 | the avoid list |
| 10, 12 | what good looks like, at the top of the skill |
| 13 | the "keep the rough edges" rule |
