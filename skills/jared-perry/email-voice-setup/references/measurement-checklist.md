# Measurement checklist

Open this at steps 2 and 4. It covers which emails go in the sample and what to count across them. Every line here ends as a number in the finished skill.

## Building the sample

Take 30 to 40 sent emails from the last one to two months. Older mail drifts: roles, clients and habits change.

Leave out:
- Auto-replies, calendar notices, receipts and anything sent by a tool on the user's behalf.
- Forwards with no added text, and replies that are one word ("Thanks!").
- Emails that are mostly quoted text from someone else.
- Mail written by an assistant or a shared inbox.

Keep the one-line replies that say something ("No problem at all, talk Thursday"). They are the most common email most people send, and leaving them out makes the voice look longer and more formal than it is.

Spread the sample across recipients. Aim for at least three emails in each kind the user sends often:

| Kind of recipient | Examples |
|---|---|
| Client, active work | updates, plans, deliverables, scope changes |
| Prospect | follow-up after a call, proposals, pricing |
| Someone they coach or manage | check-ins, feedback, missed meetings |
| Partner or boss | contract changes, reporting, asks |
| Introductions | ones they make, ones they receive, replies to the person who introduced them |
| Vendor or admin | support requests, billing, platform issues |
| Short replies inside a live thread | scheduling, reactions, quick answers |

Under 20 usable emails: mark every rule provisional and re-sweep when there are 30.

## What to count

| Measure | How to record it | What it usually decides |
|---|---|---|
| Length | words per email: median, and the share under 50 and over 300 | the default length per register |
| Greeting | each form with its count, including no greeting at all | the greeting rule, per register |
| Closer line | each line before the sign-off with its count | a short list in order of frequency |
| Sign-off | first name, full name, initials, none, with counts | when the full name appears (often vendor mail only) |
| Exclamation points | per email: zero, one, two to three, more | whether warmth is the default or the exception |
| Dashes | long dashes per email; short dashes in ranges | whether a dash in a draft is a tell |
| Paragraph length | sentences per paragraph, words per sentence | how dense a draft may get |
| Lists | numbered versus bulleted, and what each is used for | often: numbers when each item needs an answer |
| Bold | where it appears | labels, the one real ask, or never |
| Repeated phrases | any phrase in three or more emails, with its count | the phrases the skill should reach for |
| Advice verbs | "I'd", "you should", "I recommend", with counts | how the person gives advice |
| Asks | how a request is worded and whether it carries a softener | the ask pattern |
| Apologies | the first line after someone else apologises or cancels | a reflex the skill should copy exactly |
| Money | whether prices appear in email, how they are rounded | the price rule |
| Typos and run-ons | count them, do not fix them | a reason not to polish |
| Links | which booking or document links appear, and where | the verified-links list, confirmed in the interview |

## Writing findings

Write each finding with its count and its scope: "Greeting is 'Hi' or 'Hey' plus a first name in 38 of 40. 'Hey' goes to people they know, 'Hi' to new contacts and groups." Then quote one line from the mail as the example. A finding with no count is an impression, and impressions are what the skill exists to replace.
