# JTBD Clustering Reference

Use this reference when two or more prospect JTBD framings appear related but may represent distinct market segments.

## Core principle

The purpose is not to discover the most elegant common abstraction. The purpose is to identify whether prospects naturally understand the job in substantially the same way.

A difference in framing is potentially meaningful market evidence.

## The interchangeability test

Cluster two framings only when all of the following are substantially true:

1. **Same object:** They are acting on the same thing.
2. **Same action/job:** They are trying to perform essentially the same task or make the same progress.
3. **Same situational meaning:** The job arises in a sufficiently similar context that the wording can travel across conversations.
4. **Same success frame:** The buyer would recognize substantially the same definition of "done."
5. **No explanation needed:** A person from either conversation could hear the other framing and immediately say, "Yes, that's what I'm trying to do."

If one of these breaks materially, preserve the distinction.

## Three clustering outcomes

### Same cluster

Use when the difference is mostly wording.

Example:

- `Find companies that are showing signs they are actively in-market.`
- `Identify accounts displaying buying signals before they submit a form.`

These can plausibly be clustered because the job is to identify currently in-market accounts from observable signals.

### Adjacent / potentially distinct

Use when the jobs relate but the buyer's framing changes the job enough that merging may hide a segment.

Example:

- `Identify accounts displaying buying signals before they submit a form.`
- `Help SDRs decide which accounts they should call first every morning.`

The second may use intent signals, but its job is framed around daily rep prioritization. Keep separate unless the calls make their equivalence unmistakable.

### Distinct

Use when a broad category is the main thing they share.

Example:

- `Build lists of companies that match very specific conditions.`
- `Identify companies that are actively researching our category.`

Both might be served by a prospecting platform, but list construction and identifying active demand are different jobs.

## Anti-pattern: abstraction destroys evidence

Suppose four prospects say:

1. `Figure out which accounts are actively in-market.`
2. `Find companies showing intent before they hit our website.`
3. `Tell SDRs exactly who to call first each day.`
4. `Build prospect lists from unusual business signals.`

Do not collapse all four into:

`Prioritize sales opportunities`

That label is logically defensible but analytically harmful. It removes the distinctions this skill is intended to find.

A better interpretation is:

- 1 + 2: likely same cluster
- 3: adjacent / potentially distinct
- 4: distinct unless additional evidence shows otherwise

## Problem framing matters too

When available, consider how the prospect explains *why* the job is difficult or important.

Two prospects can state similar actions but reveal different market contexts through their problem framing.

Example:

- Prospect A needs to identify in-market accounts because inbound volume has collapsed.
- Prospect B needs to identify in-market accounts because a large SDR team wastes time on bad accounts.

Do not automatically split them. First determine whether the core job remains interchangeable. Record the different problem context because repeated problem-context differences may become a useful secondary segmentation signal.

## Current solution can disambiguate JTBD

If two JTBD framings seem similar, inspect what they currently use instead.

Different alternatives do not automatically mean different jobs, but they can reveal that the buyer is solving a different problem than the surface wording suggests.

Example:

- One prospect replaces a list-building database.
- Another replaces a rep-prioritization workflow in Salesforce.

That difference should increase caution before merging their JTBDs.

## How to explain a decision to the user

Keep explanations brief and educational.

Good:

`I kept “find in-market accounts” and “prioritize SDR outreach” separate. They are adjacent, but the buyer frames the job differently: one is identifying demand; the other is deciding rep action. Combining them as “sales prioritization” would erase that signal.`

Bad:

`These are different because they are different.`

Also bad:

`Both are about sales efficiency, so I merged them.`

## Confidence

Use confidence labels when helpful:

- **High:** The prospect explicitly describes the job and supporting context.
- **Medium:** The job is strongly implied but not crisply stated.
- **Low:** The interpretation relies on inference, a salesperson's paraphrase, or incomplete transcript evidence.

Low-confidence evidence should not drive aggressive clustering.
