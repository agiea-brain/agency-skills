# The feedback loop

The picks improve because yesterday's decisions are recorded and read. Without the log, day 200 is exactly as good as day 1.

## Log every pick, including the rejected ones

```
2026-10-31 | Sam Oyelaran | tariff exposure piece | SENT, edited
  edit: cut the second sentence, too explanatory
2026-10-31 | Dana Reyes | ERP post-mortem | KILLED
  reason: topical only, she's not replacing anything
2026-10-31 | Mark Liu | freight capacity | SENT, unedited
2026-10-31 | Priya Shah | lab-grown piece | HELD
  reason: good match, but contacted 9 days ago
```

Four outcomes: sent unedited, sent with edits, killed, held. Killed and held matter more than sent, because they teach the boundary.

## What each signal changes

| Signal | Tomorrow |
|---|---|
| Sent unedited | That kind of match is working. More of it. |
| Sent with edits | Record what was cut. Edits cluster, and the pattern in them is the real instruction. |
| Killed, topical only | Raise the specificity bar for that person |
| Killed, wrong tone | Adjust the note pattern for that relationship type |
| Held, too soon | Frequency working as intended. No change. |
| Replied | Strongest signal available. Note what the match was built on. |
| No reply, twice | Stop sending to that person, per the people file |

## The edits are the lesson

A thumbs down says a pick was wrong. An edit says exactly how it was wrong, which is far more useful. Keeping the before and after of an edited note, rather than just the fact of the edit, is the single highest-value thing in this log.

After a month, read the edits in one sitting. They will cluster into three or four recurring corrections, and those become fixed rules rather than per-note fixes.

## Weekly, five minutes

- How many sent, how many replied
- Which names came up more than once, and whether that is right
- Which names have not come up in two months, and whether they should have
- Any edit made more than twice, which is now a rule

## The number that matters

Replies per send, over a month. Not sends, not opens.

Under roughly one in five, the matching is generic and the fix is the people file rather than the notes. Well above that, the cap could probably rise slightly, though the discipline is usually worth more than the volume.

## What does not go in the log

Nothing the recipient said in confidence, and nothing about them that they did not share with you. The log sits alongside the people file and inherits the same obligation: it exists to help you be useful to them, and it should be something you would be comfortable with them reading.
