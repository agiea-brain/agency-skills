# The people file

The matching is only as good as this. Everything else in the skill is mechanical; this is the part that takes a month to get right and then pays out for years.

## Fields

| Field | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | | |
| `relationship` | client / prospect / partner / friend / alumni | Sets the tone and the frequency cap |
| `company and role` | VP Ops, 200-person freight brokerage | The obvious layer |
| `works on` | Replacing a TMS by Q3 | The actual matching surface |
| `cares about` | Hiring in a tight market, his daughter's hockey | What makes a send personal rather than professional |
| `competitors and customers` | Names | Their industry news is their competitor's news |
| `said` | "We're terrified of the tariff exposure" | Verbatim, with a date. The highest-value field. |
| `last contacted` | date, and whether they replied | The guardrail |
| `do not send` | Anything about layoffs | Earned the hard way |

`works on` and `said` are what separate a specific match from a topical one. A role tells you their category; a quote tells you their week.

## Building it

Do not try to fill it for 200 people. Start with 25, the ones where a well-timed note would actually land, and let it grow as conversations happen.

Sources, in order of value:

1. Things they said on calls, verbatim, with dates
2. What they post themselves
3. Their company's announcements, funding, hiring pages
4. What mutual contacts mention
5. Their title and company, which is the weakest and the one most systems stop at

## Keeping it current

Two minutes after any real conversation: update `works on`, add anything to `said`, update `last contacted`. Done weekly instead, it rots, because the specific phrasing is gone by Friday.

Once a quarter, read the file end to end. Entries whose `works on` has been the same for a year are stale, and anyone with no `said` entry has never actually been talked to.

## Frequency caps

Per relationship, sends of this kind only, not all contact.

| Relationship | Maximum | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Client, mid-engagement | Once a month | They hear from you plenty already |
| Prospect, open deal | Once a quarter, never during live negotiation | |
| Prospect, no open deal | Once every 6 weeks | |
| Partner | Once a month | |
| Friend | No cap, but it should not feel like a system | |
| Lapsed, over a year | Once, then wait for a reply | |

## The stop rule

Two sends with no response of any kind: stop sending to that person. Not a pause, a stop, until something else happens between you.

Unanswered sends do not accumulate goodwill. They accumulate evidence that you are running a program, which is the one thing this whole approach is trying not to be.
