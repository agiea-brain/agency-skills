# Matching a story to a person

## The test

Write the reason in one sentence naming something specific about them. If the sentence works for three people in the file, it is a topic, not a match, and the send is generic.

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| "She's in logistics, this is about logistics." | "She said in March they're terrified of tariff exposure on the Mexico lane. This is about exactly that lane." |
| "He's a CFO and this is a finance piece." | "He's replacing their ERP this year and this is a post-mortem on one that went badly." |
| "They'd find this interesting." | "His old company is named in paragraph four." |

## The four kinds that work

**1. Their stated problem.** Something from the `said` field, addressed directly. The strongest match available and the rarest, because it needs the file to be real.

**2. Their world, not their industry.** Their customer's industry, their competitor's move, their acquirer, their biggest account's sector. People read endlessly about their own industry and almost nothing about the industries one step away that determine their year.

**3. A person they know.** Someone they worked with, their old company, an investor, a board member, named in the piece. Nearly always worth sending, and it takes ten seconds to notice.

**4. The thing they are not paid to think about.** Their hobby, their city, their kid's sport, a book they mentioned. The least business-like and often the most effective, because it is unmistakably not a program.

## What never gets sent

- Anything about layoffs, downturns or distress in their sector, unless they raised it first
- Anything where your product or service is the obvious implied answer
- Anything critical of their company, their customers or their investors
- Anything they almost certainly read already. Their own industry's lead story is not a gift.
- Opinion pieces on politics, unless they have an explicit, standing shared interest
- Anything from a source you did not read yourself

That fourth one disqualifies more candidates than anything else. The paper's biggest story in their sector is the one piece they have definitely seen. Value comes from the pages they skipped.

## Ranking the day

Score each candidate pair:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Match is from `said`, with a date | Highest |
| Match is one step from their industry, not inside it | High |
| A person they know is named | High |
| Story is from deeper in the edition, not the front page | Moderate |
| Last contacted over 6 weeks ago | Moderate |
| Match is topical only | Disqualifying |

Take the top three to five. On a thin day, take one. The cap protects the thing that makes this work.

## The relationship branch

- **Client, mid-engagement**: must bear on their work. Anything else reads as a vendor filling space.
- **Prospect, open deal**: sparingly, never during live negotiation, and never anything adjacent to what you are selling them.
- **Partner**: the easiest sends. Referral-relevant pieces are genuinely useful to them.
- **Lapsed over a year**: half a sentence acknowledging the gap, then the article. No explanation of your absence.
- **Friend**: shorter, no framing, no sign-off that sounds professional.
