# The Replit deck prompt

Filled with findings, never with raw exports. The prompt builds the deck; the thinking happens before it.

## Fill this in

```
Build a slide deck as a single self-contained HTML page, one full-viewport
section per slide, arrow-key navigation, readable when projected.

AUDIENCE: [name, role] at [company], [category], [rough AOV].
They have not seen this before. They know their account is underperforming
and do not know by how much.

SLIDE 1 - THE NUMBER
Headline: [$X to $Y per year currently left on the table]
Subhead: one line naming where it comes from.
Footnote: the assumption behind the range, in plain language.

SLIDE 2 - WHAT WE LOOKED AT
Four lines: account scope, period reviewed, what was checked,
what was out of scope. Builds trust and prevents a later argument.

SLIDE 3, 4, 5 - ONE FINDING EACH
For each:
  - What is happening now, one sentence, factual and unarguable
  - The evidence: the specific number from their account
  - What it should be: the benchmark and its source
  - The gap: annual revenue at stake, as a range
  - The fix: one sentence, no implementation detail

SLIDE 6 - WHAT WE WOULD DO FIRST
The three fixes in order, with a rough sequence. No pricing.

SLIDE 7 - WHAT WE ARE NOT WORRYING ABOUT
Two or three things their account does well. Real ones.

DESIGN: [brand colours], [font], no stock photography, no icons for
decoration. Numbers large. One idea per slide. Dark text on light.

DO NOT: invent any number not given above, add slides, add a pricing
slide, or use the word "leverage".
```

## Why slide 7 exists

An audit that finds only problems reads as a sales document, and a prospect who has built something they are proud of stops listening. Naming two genuine strengths buys the credibility that makes the three findings land. It is also the slide that gets cut under time pressure, and it should not be.

## Why no pricing in the deck

The deck's job is agreement that the gap is real. Pricing in the same document turns the meeting into a negotiation about the price before the problem is agreed. Price after they accept the number.

## After it generates

Check every figure on every slide against the findings file. A generated deck is a draft: it will occasionally round, restate or invent a number, and one wrong figure in front of a founder who knows their account ends the meeting.

Then read it once as the prospect. If a slide would make them defensive rather than curious, rewrite the sentence, not the finding.
