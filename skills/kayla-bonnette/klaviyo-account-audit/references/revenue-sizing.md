# Sizing a finding

Every finding carries a number or it does not go in the deck. The number is what makes an audit an argument rather than a critique.

## The method

```
gap per recipient  = benchmark revenue per recipient - current revenue per recipient
annual volume      = recipients entering that flow per month x 12
revenue at stake   = gap per recipient x annual volume
```

For a missing flow, current is zero and the whole benchmark is the gap. This is why missing flows are almost always the top findings.

Use revenue per recipient, not open or click rates. It is the only number that survives a conversation with a founder.

## Benchmarks

Ranges, not points, and they move by category and average order value. Take them from the account's own comparable flows wherever possible, and from published category benchmarks otherwise. State which, on the slide.

| Flow | Typical revenue per recipient | Depends most on |
|---|---|---|
| Abandoned checkout | The highest in the account, several times any other flow | AOV and timing of the first send |
| Welcome | Strong, second or third | Whether it sells rather than introduces |
| Browse abandonment | Moderate | Catalogue size and tracking quality |
| Post-purchase | Moderate, compounding | Repurchase interval |
| Winback | Low per recipient, large volume | How the lapse window is defined |

Deliberately no hard numbers here. A benchmark table with precise figures gets quoted into a deck two years later against a different category, and a sized finding is only as good as the comparability of its benchmark. Pull current figures per engagement and cite the source.

## Honesty rules for the projection

- **State the assumption on the slide.** "Assumes checkout abandons stay at current volume and the flow reaches typical performance for this AOV band."
- **Give a range, not a point.** A single number invites an argument about that number instead of a conversation about the gap.
- **Discount for implementation.** Benchmarks come from accounts that have been optimised for a while, not from week one.
- **Never annualise a seasonal spike.** Taking Black Friday volume times twelve produces a number that destroys credibility the moment someone checks it.
- **Subtract cannibalisation on anything discount-led.** Revenue from a discount flow is not all incremental, and a sophisticated prospect knows it.

## Ranking

Sort by revenue at stake, then split by effort.

| | Low effort | High effort |
|---|---|---|
| **High revenue** | Lead the deck with these | Second section, the real project |
| **Low revenue** | Appendix, or a free quick win | Cut entirely |

The low-revenue, low-effort items are worth giving away in the meeting. Handing over two fixes for free is the cheapest credibility available and costs an hour.

## The one number on slide one

The sum of the top three findings, as an annual range, with the assumption stated underneath. Everything else in the deck exists to support that number.
