# The two numbers

Work out both before anyone scores Pay. They turn the Pay score into recognition backed by a number, and they connect the roster to the owner's goals.

## 1. The real hourly rate, per client

Average monthly revenue from the client ÷ monthly hours spent on them (hours per week × 4.33). That's what each client paid for the owner's time.

## 2. The target hourly rate, from the owner's goals

1. Take the hours a week the owner wants to work.
2. Count half of them as billable. The other half runs the business: marketing, sales, admin.
3. Multiply the billable hours by 4.33 to get monthly billable hours.
4. Divide the monthly income goal (the annual goal ÷ 12) by those hours.

That's the rate the offer has to clear for the goals to be reachable.

## Reading the gap

- **Real average below the target:** raise prices, bound scope, or get more efficient per engagement.
- **Real average above the target:** the owner is ahead and can afford to be pickier or work less.
- **Either way:** a client priced far below the target rate can't be the pattern to clone, however the other signals score.
- **A wide spread on one book usually points at scoping and terms, not the rate card.** One consultant had already raised prices hard and still ran $87-$300 an hour across the same book.

## From a real audit

A consultant with a $300,000 income goal at about 25 billable hours a week needed about $227 an hour. The flagship retainer paid $7,500 a month for about 12 hours a week, which works out to about $145 an hour. It was the biggest invoice on the book and well under the rate the goal needed.
