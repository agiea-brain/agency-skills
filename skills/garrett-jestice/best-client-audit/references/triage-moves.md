# Triage moves

## Sort, then read the signs

| Client | Real rate | Pay | Energy | Proof | Fit | Total | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | |

Sort by total, highest first. Then read the move from the pattern of signs, not just the number. A client at +1 / -3 / +3 / +1 totals +2 and lands on the fix list, and the -3 in Energy names the conversation.

| Pattern | List | Move |
|---|---|---|
| All four positive | Keep | Protect the relationship. The top total is the seed candidate |
| Exactly one negative | Fix | The negative column names the conversation (below) |
| Two negatives, or a total below zero | Exit | Offboard at the next natural boundary, like a project end or a renewal date. Never mid-engagement |

**One negative is a fix. Two is a fire.** A two-negative client isn't one conversation away from good. The owner would be renegotiating the whole relationship, and that slot is worth more empty. The lowest keeper sets the capacity bar, and every client below it is holding a seat a clone could fill.

A quick read on totals: 9 or above is clone territory, 5-8 is worth pursuing if the drag is fixable, 0-4 means something important is missing, and below 0 means stop replicating.

## The fix, by signal

- **Pay is negative:** a pricing conversation at the next renewal or project. Reprice to what the effort costs, or bound the scope back to what the price covers. It's the easiest fix on the board. If Energy and Proof are positive, they like the owner and the work works, so price is the only problem.
- **Energy is negative:** first find whose heaviness it is.
  - The owner's side (unbounded scope, custom work, no standard process): fixable. Standardize delivery, set process boundaries, and charge for change orders.
  - The client's side (a swarm of stakeholders, approval layers, a haggling culture): structural. No price covers it, so plan the exit even if they pay well.
- **Proof is negative or silent:** finish strong and ask. Close the loop on the result, name it in one sentence, then ask for a testimonial, case study, reference call or referral. Most Silent scores are unasked, not unwilling.
- **Fit is negative:** the one signal where the fix is the owner's to make. Nothing about the client's behavior is wrong; they just don't match where the business is going.
  - Adjacent (-1): move them onto the focused offer at the next boundary, or serve them out on purpose as revenue while the new business gets built, knowing they don't get cloned.
  - Misfit (-3): plan the exit at the natural boundary, even when Pay, Energy and Proof are all positive. A well-paying, easy, happy client in work the owner is leaving is the most dangerous row on the table, because every hour they hold is an hour the new business doesn't get.

What the fixes can return: in one roster, the first two fixes were a retainer renewed $1,000 a month higher with the scope narrowed, and a $7,500 back half billed in full instead of discounted. Neither one needed a new client.

## When one client carries a big share of revenue

Fix it, and never offboard it during the audit, whatever the signals say.

## When the same negative shows up across the roster

One client's negative names a conversation. The same negative across half the roster names a demand problem. The causes sit in four questions about the buyer: the Person who buys, the Project they need done, the Priority that makes it urgent now, and the Paths they could take instead.

| Roster-wide negative | Where the cause usually lives |
|---|---|
| Pay (haggling, scope creep, slow pay) | Priority or Paths. Weak urgency brings nice-to-have pricing, and open alternatives mean the price competes with "we could do this ourselves" |
| Energy (stakeholder swarms, endless signoffs) | Person: no empowered champion, or the wrong buyer in the org. Sometimes a fuzzy Project, since revisions are what an undefined scope looks like |
| Proof (silent, nothing captured) | Project or Person. The Project was never pinned to a number, so there was nothing to prove, or the sale went to the person who feels the pain instead of the one who owns the number |
| Fit | Not a demand signal. A roster full of Fit negatives means the business drifted, not the buyers |

If half the book scores Thin on Pay, the problem is who the owner sells to, not the invoicing.

## Timing

Fixes and exits happen at natural boundaries: a renewal, a project end, a scope change. Repricing mid-engagement burns trust.
