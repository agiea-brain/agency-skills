# The seed and the hypothesis

## Pick the seed

The top of the keep list is the seed candidate. A tie at the top goes to the higher Fit, then the higher Proof. Then check it three ways:

- **Lookalikes.** Find 1-2 clients on the roster like it: the same kind of company, the same problem, a similar engagement. They confirm the pick or swap it.
- **How it arrived.** A referral, inbound, the owner's own prospecting, a marketplace. A Cornerstone who arrived through a fluke intro is harder to clone than an Aligned client who came through a channel the owner can run on purpose.
- **The forward check.** Search the market on the company type and the buyer's role, and confirm enough companies match to build a pipeline on. A perfect engagement in a segment with a handful of companies is a dead end.

## Two facts to capture with the pick

These don't rank the client. They decide what gets built next.

- **Altitude.** Was the buyer succeeding with real budget, or struggling to survive? Did the urgency come from success (funding closed, the team doubled, deals stalling at the last stage) or from survival (revenue fell, a key client left)? Altitude sets the price ceiling. If the keep list mixes the two, the owner is choosing which buyer to build for, because an offer can only be framed at one altitude. A roster whose best clients were all struggling buyers is a finding on its own.
- **Problem or pains.** Did they buy against a measured business problem, a number someone owned and tracked, or against pains they had tolerated? If pains, the offer has to tie the work to a number before anything gets cloned. Otherwise the pattern being repeated is the underpriced one.

## Write the hypothesis

Fill in one sentence for the seed client:

> (Person) at (company type) had (Project) on their plate. It was urgent because (the Priority trigger). They'd tried (the Paths), and each fell short because (why). They'd be weird not to buy the thing that unblocks them.

## Test it in market

Repeatability gets proven by selling, not scoring. Take the hypothesis to market and try to close 2-3 more clients who match it.

- **Sold again 2-3 times:** the pattern is real. Rebuild the messaging, marketing and offer around it.
- **Can't sell it again:** the hypothesis was wrong, not the method. Go back to the roster, pick the next candidate, and retest.

With only 4-5 clients, this step is the whole game. Four data points can't prove a pattern, so don't pretend they do. Pick the best bet the roster supports, then let the market grade it.
