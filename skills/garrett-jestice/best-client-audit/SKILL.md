Applies when a founder-led service firm has at least four delivered clients and has to choose who to build around. Produces a scored roster, a move for every client, and the one client to clone.

## The play

1. Lay out the last 5-10 clients from the past 12-24 months. For each one, pull what they paid, what got delivered against what was scoped, how delivery felt, what happened after, and how they arrived. Check invoices and final deliverables, not memory. Memory inflates the great ones and minimizes the painful ones.
2. Run the two numbers before anyone scores: each client's real hourly rate, and the target rate the owner's income and hours goals need. The math and a worked example are in references/the-two-numbers.md.
3. Score every client on Pay, Energy, Proof and Fit, using only -3, -1, +1 or +3. Open references/scoring-rubric.md and find the row that sounds like the client, since scoring is recognition, not estimation. The owner gives every score. Bring the facts and the rubric, and ask.
4. Sort by total (-12 to +12), then read each client's move from the signs, not the total:
   - All four positive: keep. The top total is the first seed candidate.
   - Exactly one negative: fix. The negative names the conversation.
   - Two negatives, or a total below zero: exit at the next natural boundary.

   Open references/triage-moves.md for the conversation behind each negative.
5. Branch on what the roster shows:
   - The same negative on half the roster: a demand problem, not a client problem. The roster table in references/triage-moves.md says where the cause usually lives.
   - One client carries a big share of revenue: fix it, and never exit it during the audit.
   - A client priced far below the target rate: it can't be the pattern to clone, however the other signals score.
   - The best Proof and Fit sit on a client with negative Pay or Energy: check who caused the negatives. If the owner caused them by over-delivering or under-scoping, the new offer fixes them, and the client can still be the pattern. If the client caused them, keep looking.
   - Only 4-5 clients: pick the best bet the roster supports and let the market grade it.
6. Pick the seed. Ties at the top go to the higher Fit, then the higher Proof. Confirm it against 1-2 lookalikes on the roster, check that enough companies like it exist to build a pipeline on, and write the one-sentence demand hypothesis from references/seed-and-hypothesis.md.
7. Test it by selling. Try to close 2-3 more clients who match. Sold again: rebuild the offer around the pattern. Can't sell it again: the hypothesis was wrong, not the method, so go back to the roster for the next candidate.

Open references/worked-cases.md for three real audits, including one where the owner's pick broke the totals.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: the real hourly rate next to each name. It regularly reverses the story memory tells: the pleasant anchor client at $60 an hour, the demanding one quietly at $250.
- The mediocre version ranks clients by invoice size, or by who was fun to work with. Invoice size hides margin, energy and proof, and "I loved working with them" is one signal out of four.
- I know the audit worked when the winner surprises the owner. In one, a $7,500-a-month flagship lost to a small client the owner almost didn't bother scoring.
- I know the moves are right when every negative names a conversation the owner can have at the next renewal or project end.
- Red flag: the best client is the biggest logo. The biggest names are often the worst fits, with approval layers and results nobody can see. Score the logo like any other row.

## Rules

- MUST use the signed scale: -3, -1, +1 or +3. No zero, so every score forces a call.
- MUST work out each client's real hourly rate before scoring Pay.
- MUST score Proof on willingness, not occurrence. A client who would take a reference call tomorrow scores +3, even with no renewal.
- MUST grade Fit against the business the owner wants to build, not the one they run today.
- MUST time every fix and exit to a natural boundary: a renewal, a project end, a scope change.
- NEVER clone a client priced far below the target rate.
- NEVER count a missing renewal or an unasked referral against a client. Episodic work can't renew for years, and referrals nobody asked for won't come.
- NEVER compare scores across businesses. They only rank one roster.
