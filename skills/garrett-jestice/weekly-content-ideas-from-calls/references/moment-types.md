# What counts as a moment

Nine types earn their place on the list. Everything else is a topic, and topics make generic posts.

## 1. The buyer's own phrase

They describe their problem in words no marketer would choose. "We're paying for a dashboard nobody opens."
Why it works: it is search-and-scroll language. Use it verbatim in the hook.

## 2. The number they volunteered

Any figure they said without being asked: budget, headcount, hours, close rate, how long something has dragged on.
Why it works: a number in the first line stops the scroll and makes the post falsifiable.

## 3. The contradiction

They say two incompatible things in one call. "We don't have budget for this" followed by a six-figure spend on something adjacent.
Why it works: naming the pattern without naming the person is the most commented-on post shape there is.

## 4. The thing they tried that failed

A previous vendor, tool or internal attempt, and the specific reason it did not stick.
Why it works: it is the objection your whole market is carrying, said out loud.

## 5. The question they asked you

Especially a question asked twice, or asked by three different buyers this quarter.
Why it works: a question asked repeatedly is a content calendar.

## 6. The push-back

Where they disagreed with you on the call. Keep the disagreement intact.
Why it works: the post writes itself as "here is why I still think X", which is a position, not a summary.

## 7. The aside

The thing said in the first two minutes or after "anyway" that had nothing to do with the agenda.
Why it works: it is the unguarded version of their priorities.

## 8. What the client figured out

A conclusion the client reached on the call, usually mid-sentence, that they did not have when the call started. "Oh, so the problem is the handoff, not the leads."
Why it works: it is the realisation the reader is one step away from, in the words of someone who just had it.

## 9. The process you walked them through

The steps you explained when they asked how you would handle something. Not your clever line, the actual sequence: what you do first, what you check, what you skip.
Why it works: it is the most useful thing on the call and nobody else heard it. Written up, it is a how-to with proof it works in a real conversation.

## Not moments

- A restatement of their job title or company description
- Anything from the agenda slide
- Compliments about your work
- Your own clever line from the call
- Agreement

## Capture format

```
Quote: "..."
Said by: [role, company size, segment]
Prompted by: [what was being discussed]
Type: [1-9]
```

A normal week of 6 to 10 calls yields 15 to 30 candidates. Under 10 candidates usually means the week was status calls, not discovery. Widen the window to 14 days once, then stop.
