Applies on a fixed day at the end of the week, before next week's content is planned. Produces a ranked idea list built only from things real buyers said out loud.

## The play

1. Pull every call transcript from the last seven days: sales calls, client calls, partner calls, internal strategy calls where a client problem was discussed. Nothing older. Stale weeks produce generic ideas.
2. Read for moments, not topics. A moment is a sentence a buyer said that carries tension, a number, a contradiction, or a phrase in their own words. The types that keep earning, with examples, are in references/moment-types.md. Expect 15 to 30 candidate moments from a normal week and far fewer from a week of status calls.
3. Pull each one as a verbatim quote with who said it (role, not name) and what prompted it. Paraphrase at this stage and the idea goes flat by Tuesday.
4. Score every candidate against the ranking model in references/ranking-model.md. The metric is comments per 1,000 impressions on past posts, not likes and not raw impressions: it is the only one that tracks whether the audience had something to say back. Build the baseline once from the last 90 days of posts and reuse it.
5. Cut to 8 to 12 ideas. Fewer than 8 means the week's calls were too thin, so widen to 14 days once and say so. More than 12 and nothing gets written well.
6. For each surviving idea write: the quote, a hook line, and two formats it could be written in. Two formats, not one, because the same moment lands differently as a story than as a list, and the choice is made at drafting time.
7. Flag the top 3. These are the ones that get written if the week collapses. Branch by what the week produced:
   - A moment repeated by two or more different buyers goes straight to the top 3, whatever it scores.
   - A single client's very specific situation scores well but is not a top 3 pick unless the pattern shows up elsewhere.
   - A moment that contradicts something already published is always a top 3 pick. Those are the posts that get comments.
8. Close with 6 to 8 voice questions, drawn from references/voice-questions.md and pointed at the top 3. These are what the writer answers before drafting, so the post carries a real position instead of a summary of the call.

## What good looks like

- First thing I check that most people skip: whether the quote would survive being read aloud to the person who said it. If it would embarrass them, it is not usable, however good the hook is.
- The mediocre version: a list of topics lifted from call summaries. "Clients are struggling with attribution." True, generic, already posted by everyone. The quote is what makes it specific, and the mediocre version drops it.
- I know the output is right when a writer can open the list cold on Monday and start typing without listening to a single call.
- Second test: at least two of the ideas make the writer slightly uncomfortable. Comfortable ideas get no comments.
- Red flag: the top 3 are all from the same call. That is one conversation, not a week of signal.

## Rules

- MUST keep every idea anchored to a verbatim quote from an actual call.
- MUST strip client and company names before anything is published, keeping role, size and situation.
- MUST rank on comments per 1,000 impressions, never on likes or follower count.
- MUST give each idea two formats.
- MUST run on the same day every week. The cadence is what makes the backlog compound.
- NEVER publish a quote that identifies the speaker without their explicit yes.
- NEVER carry an unused idea forward more than two weeks. If it has not been written by then, it was not an idea.
