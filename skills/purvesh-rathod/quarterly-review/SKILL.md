Applies to the quarterly or annual review where a client decides whether the retainer continues. Produces the agenda, the narrative, the forward plan and the executive one-pager.

## The play

1. Find what was committed at the last QBR before building anything. Every one of those promises gets accounted for in this deck as delivered, partly delivered, or not. A client who spots an unaddressed promise stops trusting every other slide, and they do spot it.

2. Open on the client's business, not the firm's work. What were they trying to do this quarter, what moved in their market, where did they land. If a competitor got aggressive or their funding changed, it goes here, early. This is the section that separates a partner from a vendor, and most decks skip straight past it to the dashboard.

3. Then show the quarter's results, each one connected to something the client said they wanted. A result that maps to nothing the client cares about does not belong in the main deck. It can go in an appendix.

4. Pick three wins and give each one its cause, so the win reads as repeatable rather than lucky. Three with reasoning beats nine as a list.

5. Take the misses head on: what underperformed, the actual reason, and what changes next quarter. A deck claiming everything worked is the one that triggers a review, because the client already knows what did not and is now wondering what else is being managed.

6. Build the forward plan as three to five recommendations, each with its reasoning and what it needs. Include at least one thing the firm thinks the client should do that the firm is not paid for. That is the specific move that reads as partnership rather than account growth.

7. Make the expansion case only if the quarter earned it, and make it on the client's goals rather than the firm's capacity. A weak expansion ask in a weak quarter costs more than it can win.

8. Write the agenda with timings, built around who is in the room and when each person leaves.

9. Write the one-pager for the senior attendee last: where the quarter landed, the one risk, the one ask. It has to make sense to someone who reads nothing else and leaves after ten minutes.

## Rules

- MUST account for every commitment made at the last review.
- MUST open on the client's business context, not on performance.
- MUST connect each headline result to a goal the client stated.
- MUST address the quarter's misses with a cause and a correction.
- MUST include one recommendation outside the firm's paid scope.
- MUST write the one-pager so it stands alone.
- NEVER state a result not supplied by the owner or a connected source.
- NEVER open with metrics.
- NEVER make an expansion case in a quarter that underdelivered.
