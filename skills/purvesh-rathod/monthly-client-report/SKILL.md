Applies to the recurring report a retainer client receives. Produces the report itself, written so the client can tell what the firm thinks, not just what happened.

## The play

1. Get the prior periods before writing a word. Without them there is no trend, only a number, and a report that asserts a trend it cannot show is the fastest way to lose a client's trust in the whole document.

2. Decide which metrics belong in the report, and cut the rest. A report containing everything signals the firm has not decided what matters. Cutting a metric is a real decision and it is worth making deliberately, including telling the client you cut it.

3. Write the summary last and put it first. Three or four sentences: where the period landed against target, the one thing that moved most, the one thing changing next month. Assume this is all a busy client reads, because usually it is.

4. Build the number table as metric, this period, last period, target, status. Plain status words, not colour alone.

5. Explain each meaningful move, and this is the whole reason the report exists. Not that impressions fell twelve percent, but why, and whether it matters. Branch honestly:
   - Worse and it matters: say what is being done, with a date.
   - Worse and it does not matter: say why not, specifically. A client who is told not to worry without a reason worries more.
   - Better on purpose: say what caused it, so it reads as repeatable.
   - Better by accident: say that too. Claiming credit for luck is how a firm ends up with a target it cannot hit again.

6. Put in what the numbers cannot show. A paused campaign, a two-week asset delay, a change the client requested that cost performance. This goes in the report, not in a reply after the client asks. The firm that volunteers the awkward thing is the one that gets believed on the good months.

7. Write one recommendation per channel or workstream, specific enough that the client could disagree with it. A recommendation nobody could argue with is not a recommendation.

8. Close with what the firm needs from the client, with dates. Then match the firm's reporting voice, and strip firm branding if the report goes out white-labeled.

## Rules

- MUST have the prior period in hand before describing any trend.
- MUST state a number only from a supplied or connected source.
- MUST give a reason for every meaningful move, including the good ones.
- MUST report anything that affected the period and does not appear in the numbers.
- MUST write one arguable recommendation per workstream.
- NEVER write "performance was strong". Name the metric, the comparison, and the cause.
- NEVER soften a bad month into ambiguity.
- NEVER claim a result the firm caused by accident as a deliberate win.
