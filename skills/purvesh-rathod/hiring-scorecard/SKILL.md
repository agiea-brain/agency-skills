Applies to any role, before the first interview. Produces the scorecard and the questions that fill it in, not a job description.

## The play

1. Write what the person has to be able to do in their first six months, as outcomes rather than responsibilities. "Ship the integration layer without supervision" is an outcome. "Strong engineering skills" is a feeling.

2. Cut the list to four or five. A scorecard with ten dimensions is scored by nobody, and the panel reverts to overall impression, which is the thing the scorecard exists to replace.

3. For each one, write what evidence would actually count. Past work you can inspect, a decision they made and why, a specific thing they built. Then write what does not count: fluency, enthusiasm, having worked somewhere impressive, and agreeing with the interviewer.

4. Write the question that produces that evidence. Ask about a specific past instance rather than an approach, because asked in general people describe best practice and asked about last time they describe what they actually do.

5. Add one exercise where the role allows it, scoped to under ninety minutes and resembling the real work. Review it against the same dimensions as everything else rather than as a separate impression.

6. Set the bar per dimension before the first conversation: what a no looks like, what a yes looks like, and which dimensions cannot be compensated for by a strength elsewhere. Deciding this after meeting a likeable candidate is how a scorecard becomes paperwork.

7. Split the dimensions across the panel so two people are not both assessing the same thing while nobody covers the third. Say who owns which.

8. Require the scores to be written before the panel talks to each other. Once the first person says they liked them, everyone else's independent read is gone.

## Rules

- MUST write first-six-months outcomes, not responsibilities.
- MUST keep the scorecard to four or five dimensions.
- MUST state what evidence counts and what does not for each dimension.
- MUST ask about specific past instances rather than general approach.
- MUST set the bar per dimension before meeting anyone.
- MUST assign dimensions to named panel members.
- MUST collect written scores before the panel discusses the candidate.
- NEVER add a dimension without the question that produces evidence for it.
- NEVER let a strength on one dimension cover a no on a dimension marked as non-negotiable.
