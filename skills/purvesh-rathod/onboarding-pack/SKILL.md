Applies in the days between a signature and the kickoff call. Produces the week-one document set, not the recurring check-in that follows it.

## The play

1. Read everything from the sale before writing anything. The proposal, the call notes, the thread. What the client said they wanted, and what they said about whoever had the work before, is the raw material for every document in the pack. Onboarding built without it restates the contract back to them.

2. Write the kickoff agenda against a real meeting length with time on each block. Open on the client's own goal in their words, not on introductions. Put access handoff and the approval path in the agenda explicitly. Those two are what quietly eat month one, and they get skipped when the agenda is written to feel welcoming rather than to get things decided.

3. Cut the discovery questions down to what is actually still open. Drop anything already answered in the sale, anything that is true of every client in their industry, and anything the firm could look up in ten minutes. State at the top of the questionnaire which questions were dropped and why. A client asked twenty questions the firm could have answered itself concludes nobody read the proposal.

4. Build the access list platform by platform: what is needed, at what permission level, who grants it, and the date it blocks if it is late. Send it the day the contract is signed, not at kickoff. Access is the single most common reason a first month produces nothing.

5. Write the 30-60-90 as commitments. Each phase gets named deliverables and the one thing that will be observably true at the end of it. If a phase cannot carry a real commitment, say so rather than filling it with setup tasks dressed as milestones.

6. Settle the decisions that cause friction later, in a short table: who approves work, who can move budget, who gets escalated to, and how fast each side replies. Four rows beats a process document nobody opens.

7. Draft the welcome email the account lead actually sends. Short, in the firm's voice, with the two or three things the client has to do before kickoff.

8. Write the internal brief last and keep it internal: what was sold, what was promised verbally and is not in the contract, where the sensitivities are, and what would make this account grow. The verbal promises are the part worth writing down, because they are what the client remembers and the contract does not hold.

## Rules

- MUST read the sale record before drafting any document in the pack.
- MUST drop discovery questions already answered elsewhere, and say which were dropped.
- MUST send the access list on signature, not at kickoff.
- MUST write each phase of the 30-60-90 as a named commitment with an observable outcome.
- MUST capture verbal promises from the sale in the internal brief.
- NEVER invent a commitment the firm has not agreed to deliver. Ask, or leave a visible blank.
- NEVER pad the questionnaire to look thorough.
